# Module 12 — Foundation

**Draft v1, after one review pass — 15 Nov 2026 — Not yet reviewed by a builder, engineer, or inspector. Do not build from this draft.**

Markers: **[V]** verified against a cited source on 15 Nov 2026. **[E]** estimate or common trade practice, not sourced. Where this module and the stamped plans disagree, the plans govern.

**Outcome:** when you finish this module you will have an inspected concrete foundation that is square, level, and at the right place and height, with every anchor, sleeve, and opening the later trades need.

**Assumed build**

- Unincorporated Kitsap County, permit issued, plans approved.
- About 2,000 sq ft single story on a crawl space: continuous concrete footings, concrete stem walls about 2 ft tall, interior pier pads.
- Two pours: footings first, walls second.
- Gently sloping lot, no geotechnical conditions, septic and utilities already located (Module 11).
- A slab-on-grade or basement foundation changes steps 12.3, 12.14, 12.20, and 12.23 and needs its own version.

**Before you start**

- Approvals: building permit issued; erosion control installed.
- Documents on site: stamped plans, permit, structural sheets, hold-down schedule, truss/floor layout, plumbing and electrical service plans.
- From Module 11: surveyed corners or offset stakes, rough excavation, site access for a concrete truck and pump.
- People: two to four workers on pour days; engineer reachable by phone.

**Design values to pull from your stamped plans (do not assume)**

- Footing width and thickness, wall thickness and height.
- Rebar size, spacing, laps, and grade.
- Anchor bolt size and spacing; hold-down models and locations.
- Soil bearing value, frost depth, and seismic design category. Kitsap County Code 14.04 adopts Seismic Design Category D2, a 12 in. frost depth, 25 psf snow load, 110 mph ultimate wind speed, and moderate weathering [V]. Soil bearing is normally assumed at 1,500 psf unless a geotechnical report supports more [E]. Confirm all of these on your plans.

**Sequence**

| Steps | Work | Waits on |

| 12.1–12.4 | Plan, schedule embeds, choose method, order | Lead times: rebar, forms, hold-down hardware |
| 12.5–12.10 | Layout, excavate, form footings, steel, grounding | — |
| 12.11 | Footing and setback inspection | Inspector |
| 12.12 | Pour footings | Weather, pump |
| 12.13–12.15 | Form walls, steel, sleeves, embeds | — |
| 12.16 | Foundation wall inspection | Inspector |
| 12.17–12.19 | Pour walls, cure, strip, as-built check | Cure time |
| 12.20–12.25 | Drainage, crawl space water, backfill, garage slab if any, closeout | Drainage inspection |

**Inspections:** erosion control and footing/setback (12.11); foundation wall (12.16); foundation drainage where required (12.20); under-slab inspections if a garage slab is poured (12.24); special inspection only if the plans or a repair call for it. The county's initial erosion control inspection can be requested together with footing and setback [V].

**Budget lines [E — replace with bids]:** rebar and hardware; form rental or lumber; concrete (two deliveries); pump (two visits); gravel and drain pipe; dampproofing; engineer site visit if needed. Concrete and pump are normally paid on delivery.

**Points of no return:** footing pour (12.12) fixes the building's position; wall pour (12.17) fixes every embed and opening.

**Stop and call the engineer when:** the soil at footing grade is soft, wet, filled, or organic; any footing, wall, or pier pad cannot be built as drawn; a hold-down or anchor bolt is missed or more than 1/2 in. off; honeycombing exposes rebar or a wall has a cold joint; a wall cracks or moves after backfill; you want to core a hole through a footing or within 12 in. of a hold-down; or you want to substitute any hardware, bar grade, or concrete mix [E].

---

### 12.1 Review the foundation plan against every sheet
- **What:** a marked-up foundation plan with every conflict found and resolved before any dirt moves.
- **How:** 1) Overlay the foundation plan on the floor framing, plumbing, and electrical plans. 2) Check that beam pockets, pier pads, and bearing walls line up with framing above. 3) Check overall dimensions add up along each wall. 4) List questions and send them to the designer or engineer in one message.
- **Standard:** zero unresolved conflicts (craft).
- **Crew, time:** 1 person, 2–4 hours.
- **Output:** marked-up plan and the engineer's written answers.
- **Done when:** every dimension string closes and every question has a written answer.
- **If it goes wrong:** a conflict found later costs a plan revision. A change to the approved plans needs a permit revision.

### 12.2 Build the embed and penetration schedule
- **What:** one list of everything that must be cast into or pass through the concrete.
- **How:** walk each trade's plan and list item, location, size, and height: anchor bolts, hold-downs, plumbing sleeves (water in, sewer out), electrical conduit sleeves, grounding electrode, crawl space vents, access opening (at least 16 in. x 24 in. through a wall, 18 in. x 24 in. through the floor [V]), beam pockets (beam width plus 1/2 in. air space each side and at the end, bearing height taken from the framing section [E]), a low-point drain sleeve through the footing or wall where the crawl space floor will sit below outside grade (12.20), crossings for the footing drain outfall and downspout lines, hose-bib and gas sleeves, radon provisions if applicable.
- **Do now for later:** this list is the step. Ask the plumber and electrician to mark it up, even if you will do that work yourself.
- **Inspector checks:** sleeves for plumbing and electrical through the wall or footing [V].
- **Local note:** state radon construction requirements apply in seven listed counties; Kitsap is not on the list [V]. Confirm whether an unvented crawl space design triggers them.
- **Crew, time:** 1 person, 2–3 hours plus trade input.
- **Output:** embed schedule in the binder; copy taped inside the plan set.
- **Done when:** each trade has initialed the list.
- **If it goes wrong:** a missed sleeve means core drilling cured concrete, and possibly an engineer's approval for the hole.

### 12.3 Choose the forming and pour method
- **What:** a written decision on who forms and pours, and how.
- **Options [E]:**

| Option | Cost | Time | Skill | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Hire a foundation subcontractor for all of it | Highest | 1–2 weeks | Low | Low; schedule depends on the sub |
| B. You form and place steel; hire a finisher and pump for pour days | Medium | 3–5 weeks part-time | Medium | Medium |
| C. Do it all with rented form panels | Lower | 4–6 weeks part-time | High | Blowouts, out-of-level walls |
| D. Insulated concrete forms | Higher material | 3–5 weeks | Medium | Bracing and consolidation |

- **Recommendation:** B for a first build. You capture most of the labor savings and put experienced hands on the two days that cannot be redone.
- **Track note:** on a house built for sale, your own labor falls under the contractor rules in Module 2. Decide A unless you are registered.
- **Done when:** method chosen, quotes in hand for the hired parts.

### 12.4 Order materials and book pour-day services
- **What:** confirmed orders and dates for rebar, forms, hardware, concrete, and pump.
- **How:** 1) Take off rebar lengths, add laps and about 10% waste [E]. 2) Order hold-downs and anchor bolts by the exact model on the plans. 3) Reserve form panels. 4) Open an account with a ready-mix supplier; give them the mix required by the plans. 5) Book the pump tentatively for both pours.
- **Standard:** concrete strength and air content per plans. Washington checklists call for at least 3,000 psi and 5–7% air entrainment for walls exposed to weather [V]. That is a durability floor, not your design: order the strength, slump, and air content written in the structural notes, and keep every delivery ticket. If the notes call for concrete strength testing or special inspection, book the testing agency before you book the truck [E]. Do not let anyone add water at the site beyond what the ticket allows; it lowers strength [E].
- **Money out:** hardware and rebar now; concrete and pump on delivery.
- **Who:** ready-mix dispatcher. Ask: minimum load, short-load fee, lead time, Saturday delivery, washout rules.
- **Done when:** every order has a confirmation and a date.
- **If it goes wrong:** special-order hold-downs arrive late. Order them first.

### 12.5 Establish layout lines
- **What:** batter boards and string lines marking the outside face of the foundation.
- **Options:** A. Surveyor stakes the building corners (few hundred dollars and up [E], lowest risk). B. You lay out from surveyed property corners with a total station or tapes. Recommended: A when any wall is within a few feet of a setback or buffer; B otherwise, checked by diagonals.
- **How:** 1) Set batter boards 4–6 ft outside each corner. 2) Pull strings for outside wall faces. 3) Measure both diagonals. 4) Adjust until equal.
- **Standard:** diagonals within 1/4 in.; wall lengths within 1/8 in. (craft) [E].
- **Crew, time, tools:** 2 people, half a day; builder's level or laser, 100-ft tape, stakes.
- **Done when:** diagonals match and each string is labeled.
- **If it goes wrong:** strings get knocked. Mark their positions on the batter boards with saw kerfs so they can be reset.

### 12.6 Verify setbacks and set the top-of-foundation elevation
- **What:** measured setbacks recorded, and one benchmark elevation for the top of wall.
- **How:** 1) Measure from property lines, easements, and buffers to the strings; compare to the approved site plan. 2) Pick top-of-wall height so finished grade can slope away, the sewer or septic line has fall, and the driveway works. 3) Mark a benchmark on a stake or tree outside the work area.
- **Standard:** top of wall at least 6 in. above finished grade, 4 in. with masonry veneer [V]. Ground falls at least 6 in. in the first 10 ft from the foundation [V].
- **Inspector checks:** footings and foundation outside setbacks, critical areas, and easements [V].
- **Do now for later:** this elevation sets floor height, step count at every door, and garage slab height.
- **Done when:** setbacks are written on the site plan and the benchmark is marked.
- **If it goes wrong:** foundation set too low is the classic drainage failure and cannot be fixed after the pour.

### 12.7 Excavate footings to bearing soil
- **What:** trenches at plan depth and width on firm, undisturbed soil.
- **How:** 1) Dig to grade using the laser. 2) Hand-clean the bottom. 3) Step the trench where the ground slopes. 4) Dig interior pier pads.
- **Standard:** bottom level, or stepped where the ground slopes more than 1 ft in 10 ft [V]. Depth at or below frost depth per plans.
- **Inspector checks:** subgrade firm and undisturbed; loose soil, mud, water, and organics removed. Over-excavated or filled areas need a compaction report from a special inspector or geotechnical engineer [V].
- **Weather and safety:** call for utility locates before digging; keep spoils back from trench edges; pump out standing water.
- **Crew, time, tools:** 1 operator and 1 grade checker, 1–2 days; excavator with a smooth bucket, laser.
- **Done when:** every trench bottom reads on grade and is clean.
- **If it goes wrong:** you dig too deep. Do not refill with loose soil. Fill the extra depth with concrete, or get the engineer's direction.

### 12.8 Form the footings
- **What:** forms set to line and level at plan width and thickness.
- **How:** stake 2x form boards to the strings, set tops to the benchmark, brace every 2–3 ft, add step bulkheads.
- **Standard:** minimum 12 in. wide and 6 in. thick, or as the plans require; footing projects at least 2 in. past the wall face and no more than its own thickness [V]. Form tops level within 1/4 in. (craft) [E].
- **Crew, time:** 2 people, 1–2 days.
- **Pier pads:** form the interior pier pads at the same time. Their size, thickness, and reinforcing come from the point loads on the plans, not a generic square. Set the tops to the beam and girder heights and place any cast-in post bases now [E].
- **Done when:** laser reads level around the full perimeter and diagonals still match.

### 12.9 Place footing rebar and vertical dowels
- **What:** all footing steel tied in position, with dowels standing where the walls will be.
- **How:** 1) Set horizontal bars on chairs or dobies. 2) Tie laps. 3) Tie hooked vertical dowels at plan spacing along the wall centerline. 4) Cap exposed bar ends.
- **Standard:** cover of 3 in. where concrete is cast against earth; 1-1/2 in. for #5 and smaller bars exposed to earth or weather; bend diameter at least 6 bar diameters; two ties per lap; bars clean [V]. Kitsap is Seismic Design Category D2, where the code requires reinforcing in footings and stem walls. The prescriptive minimum is one No. 4 horizontal bar 3 to 4 in. above the footing bottom, one No. 4 horizontal bar within 12 in. of the top of the stem wall, and No. 4 vertical dowels no more than 48 in. apart, each with a standard hook in the footing, 3 in. clear of the footing bottom, and reaching at least 14 in. up into the stem wall [V]. That is the code floor, not a design: stamped plans usually show more steel, and the plans govern. Lap bars to the length on the plans; if the plans are silent ask the engineer, and expect about 30 in. for a No. 4 Grade 60 bar [E]. Plans in this seismic category normally call for Grade 60 bar; do not substitute Grade 40 [E]. Dowels are tied in place before the footing inspection, never pushed into wet concrete, and bars are never heated or re-bent after the pour [E].
- **Inspector checks:** grade, size, spacing, splices, and cover against plans [V].
- **Weather and safety:** impalement caps on every vertical bar.
- **Crew, time, tools:** 2 people, 1 day; rebar cutter/bender, tie wire, chairs.
- **Done when:** a tape check at ten random spots matches plan spacing and cover.
- **If it goes wrong:** steel sitting on dirt. It must be supported, not pulled up during the pour.

### 12.10 Install the grounding electrode
- **What:** the concrete-encased electrode (often called a Ufer ground) in the footing, with a stub brought up at the future electrical service location.
- **How:** follow the electrical plan for location and method; leave the connection point accessible and tagged.
- **Do now for later:** the electrician and the state electrical inspector need this. It cannot be added after the pour.
- **Who:** the state electrical inspector inspects the electrode, not the county. State rule requires a concrete-encased electrode at every new building on a permanent concrete foundation [V]. Choose one of two routes: request a state inspection on your electrical permit while the electrode is still visible, before the pour; or attach a copper test lead to one end of the electrode, long enough to reach the service location, so the inspector can verify continuity over a span of at least 20 ft at the service inspection [V]. A special trip to inspect it carries a trip fee [V]. Buy the electrical permit (5.11) before this step and tell the inspector's office which route you are using.
- **Track note:** on a house built for sale this is licensed electrical work (Module 2).
- **Status:** [V] requirement and inspection routes checked against the state electrical rule (WAC 296-46B-250). Electrode material, length, and connection details come from the electrical code and are not verified here [E].
- **If it goes wrong:** the electrode is missed or cannot be proven. The fallback is a ground ring or another electrode proven by an independent test firm at 25 ohms or less [V]. That costs far more than getting it seen before the pour.
- **Done when:** electrode is in, photographed, and the electrical inspector's requirement is met.

### 12.11 Pass the footing and setback inspection (wait)
- **What:** signed approval to pour footings.
- **How:** 1) Run the county footing checklist yourself the day before. 2) Request the inspection through the permit portal, with erosion control if not yet done. 3) Have address posted, permit and approved plans on site [V].
- **Inspector checks:** excavation complete with forms and required reinforcement in place [V]; setbacks; subgrade; steel; any special inspector reports the plans require [V].
- **Standard:** the county checklist is still labeled to the 2018 code [V]. Ask the inspector which edition your permit is under.
- **Done when:** inspection is approved in the portal. Do not schedule concrete until it is.
- **If it goes wrong:** a correction notice. Fix, photograph, and re-request. Never pour on a failed or missing inspection.

### 12.12 Pour the footings
- **What:** footings placed, consolidated, and struck off level.
- **How:** 1) Confirm truck and pump access and a washout spot. 2) Dampen dry soil. 3) Place continuously around the perimeter. 4) Consolidate around bars and dowels. 5) Strike off to form tops. 6) Leave the top rough or form a keyway where the wall will sit. 7) Re-plumb dowels before the concrete sets.
- **Weather and safety:** cold weather needs protection and frost-free forms, steel, and ground; hot weather needs precautions against rapid drying [V]. Do not pour in standing water. Wear eye protection, gloves, and boots; wet concrete burns skin.
- **Crew, time, tools:** 3–4 people, half a day; pump, vibrator, screed, rakes.
- **Money out:** concrete and pump, on delivery.
- **Done when:** tops are level to the forms and every dowel is plumb and in line.
- **If it goes wrong:** you run short. Order about 5–10% over the calculated volume [E] and know the supplier's short-load time before you start.

### 12.13 Snap wall lines and set wall forms
- **What:** forms for the stem walls set plumb, straight, and to height.
- **How:** 1) Reset strings; snap wall lines on the footing. 2) Re-check diagonals on the footing. 3) Set one side of the forms. 4) Leave the other side open for steel and embeds. 5) Coat form faces with release agent before steel goes in, never after. 6) Install ties and braces to the form supplier's layout; ask the rental yard for it in writing. 7) Shoot the top-of-wall elevation with the laser and mark it inside the forms with a chalk line or a nailed grade strip; do not assume the form tops are the pour line [E].
- **Standard:** wall thickness per plans; plumb within 1/8 in. over a 2 ft wall; top line straight within 1/4 in. along the wall (craft) [E].
- **Crew, time:** 2–3 people, 2–3 days with rented panels.
- **If it goes wrong:** the footing is slightly out of square. Correct it now by shifting the wall line within the footing, keeping at least 2 in. of projection. The dowels are already cast, so check that each one still has at least 1-1/2 in. of concrete between it and the form face after the shift. If the shift is more than about 1 in., if any dowel loses cover, or if a hold-down location moves, stop and get the engineer's written direction. Do not bend dowels that are cast in concrete to make them fit [E].

### 12.14 Place wall steel, sleeves, and openings
- **What:** horizontal bars tied to the dowels, and every sleeve, vent, access opening, and beam pocket from the embed schedule boxed in.
- **How:** work the embed schedule line by line; fix each item to the form so the pour cannot move it; close the second form side; brace.
- **Standard:** crawl space vents and access per plans and code section R408; wall thickness per plans [V]. The state amendment requires net vent area of at least 1 sq ft per 300 sq ft of underfloor area, placed for cross ventilation; operable louvers, or less vent area than that, trigger a radon vent under the ground cover [V]. An access opening through the perimeter wall is at least 16 in. x 24 in. [V].
- **Do now for later:** plumber (water and sewer sleeves), electrician (service conduit), framer (beam pockets, girder heights), HVAC (any line-set sleeve).
- **Crew, time:** 2 people, 1–2 days.
- **Done when:** every line on the embed schedule is checked off and photographed.

### 12.15 Mount hold-down anchors and anchor-bolt templates
- **What:** every hold-down anchor fixed in the forms at its exact location; every anchor bolt position marked.
- **How:** 1) Mark hold-down locations from a building corner, not from the previous anchor. 2) Hang each from a template across the form top. 3) Set embedment and projection to the hardware maker's table. 4) Tie it off the rebar. 5) Mark anchor bolt positions on the form, or better, hang the bolts now from wood or manufactured bolt holders across the form top. Wet-set bolts lean, sink, and get voids around them; pre-hung bolts are inspected in place [E]. 6) Before buying bolts, read the shear wall schedule for bolt diameter and sill plate thickness: some shear walls call for a 3x sill, which needs a longer bolt to keep 7 in. of embedment. Set projection to the plate thickness plus about 1 in. for washer and nut: about 2-1/2 in. for a 2x sill and 3-1/2 in. for a 3x [E].
- **Standard:** the code floor for anchor bolts is 1/2 in. diameter, no more than 6 ft apart, at least 7 in. into concrete, in the middle third of the plate width, at least two per plate piece with one no more than 12 in. and no less than 7 bolt diameters from each end [V]. Engineered shear walls in this seismic category usually call for 5/8 in. bolts at closer spacing that changes from wall to wall, so lay out each wall from the shear wall schedule, never from the 6 ft rule [E]. In Seismic Design Category D2 a plate washer at least 3 in. x 3 in. x 0.229 in. goes on every anchor bolt along a braced or shear wall line [V]. Check the plans for 3x sill plates and for bolts offset toward the sheathed face, and set bolt projection and position to suit [E]. Location within about 1/4 in. (craft) [E]. Hold-down anchors follow the maker's sheet. For the common cast-in hold-down bolt that means in place before the pour, at least 1-3/4 in. from the concrete edge, a No. 4 bar 3 to 5 in. below the top of the wall, concrete of at least 2,500 psi, and a wall at least 6 in. thick, or 8 in. for the 7/8 in. bolt [V]. Never cut, bend, or weld a hold-down anchor or swap one model for another without the engineer's written approval [E].
- **Inspector checks:** anchor bolts may be set in wet concrete; hold-down straps and their embedded bolts may not. Hold-downs per stamped plans and maker's instructions [V].
- **Do now for later:** bolts must miss door openings, plate splices, and stud locations where posts or hold-downs land. Lay out against the wall framing plan.
- **Crew, time:** 1–2 people, half a day.
- **Done when:** the hold-down schedule is fully checked off with photos showing a tape.
- **If it goes wrong:** a misplaced hold-down found after the pour needs the engineer's written fix. Anchors installed into cured concrete (epoxy or wedge) require special inspection unless the engineer waives it and the county approves [V]. The engineer picks the product, diameter, embedment, and edge distance; a retrofit anchor near the edge or end of a wall often holds far less than the cast-in bolt it replaces [E]. Adhesive anchors need concrete at least 21 days old [V], a hole drilled, brushed, and blown clean exactly as the maker's instructions say, and the special inspector on site while you install, not afterward [E].

### 12.16 Pass the foundation wall inspection (wait)
- **What:** signed approval to pour walls.
- **How:** self-check against the county checklist, request inspection, keep one form face open or accessible so steel and embeds are visible.
- **Inspector checks:** wall thickness, steel, cover, hold-downs, vents, sleeves, height above grade [V].
- **Done when:** approved in the portal.

### 12.17 Pour the walls and set anchor bolts
- **What:** walls placed in lifts, consolidated, top struck level, anchor bolts set.
- **How:** 1) Place in lifts around the perimeter, not all in one spot. 2) Vibrate each lift, briefly, and around every embed. 3) Watch forms for movement; stop if anything shifts. 4) Strike the top to the level line. 5) Set anchor bolts at the marks right after strike-off, while the concrete is still plastic, and vibrate or rod around each one; never force a bolt into concrete that has started to stiffen. Bolts hung from templates before the pour are more accurate and are the better practice [E]. Hold-down anchors are never set this way (12.15). 6) Re-check hold-downs for plumb.
- **Standard:** top of wall level within 1/4 in. across the building (craft) [E]. Where bolts resist placement, vibrate so concrete fully surrounds them [V].
- **Weather and safety:** same limits as 12.12. Keep people clear of the pump boom and away from form faces during placement.
- **Crew, time, tools:** 4 people, half a day; pump, vibrator, laser, floats.
- **Money out:** concrete and pump.
- **Done when:** top is level, bolts are plumb with correct projection, forms held.
- **If it goes wrong:** a form bulges or blows out. Stop the pump, brace or shovel out, re-form. Have spare braces, stakes, and plywood staged before the truck arrives.

### 12.18 Cure and protect (wait)
- **What:** concrete kept moist and protected until it gains strength.
- **How:** leave forms on at least a day [E]; cover or mist in dry weather; insulate in cold.
- **Standard:** follow the plans or the supplier's guidance for curing time. Where they are silent, keep the concrete moist and above about 50°F for 7 days and never let it freeze in the first days [E]. Until at least 7 days have passed, longer in cold weather, do not tighten anchor bolt or hold-down nuts, stack lumber on the walls, or backfill [E].
- **Done when:** the curing period on your plan or from your supplier has passed.

### 12.19 Strip forms and measure the as-built foundation
- **What:** an as-built drawing with actual lengths, diagonals, top-of-wall elevations, and bolt locations.
- **How:** 1) Strip and clean forms; return rentals. 2) Measure every wall and both diagonals. 3) Shoot the top of wall at each corner and every 8–10 ft. 4) Check each hold-down against the plan.
- **Standard:** if the foundation is out by more than about 1/2 in. in square or level [E], decide the correction with the framer before lumber is cut.
- **Do now for later:** give the framer the as-built. They correct small errors at the mudsill.
- **Inspector checks:** honeycombing or exposed steel may draw a correction.
- **Done when:** as-built is in the binder and shared with the framer.

### 12.20 Install dampproofing and foundation drainage where required (wait)
- **What:** exterior coating and footing drain as the plans and site require.
- **How:** apply coating to the exterior below grade; lay perforated pipe in washed rock with filter fabric, sloped to daylight or an approved discharge; keep roof drain lines separate.
- **Standard:** below-grade habitable space requires waterproofing or dampproofing, checked at the drainage inspection [V]. Crawl space requirements depend on soil and site; follow the plans. Where groundwater can rise near the floor or surface water does not drain away, the model code requires the crawl space floor to be as high as outside grade unless an approved drainage system is provided [V]. Most crawl spaces here are dug below outside grade, so plan on a footing drain and a crawl space low-point drain to daylight unless the plans and the inspector say neither is needed [E]. A crawl space that holds winter water is the most common foundation complaint in this region and is very costly to fix after backfill.
- **Inspector checks:** the county's Drainage/Waterproofing inspection, before backfill: footing and downspout drains located and routed to their connection or termination point, drain rock around the footing drain pipe with filter fabric on top, and waterproofing where interior space is below grade [V].
- **Done when:** inspection approved and photos taken before cover.
- **If it goes wrong:** backfilled before inspection. You dig it up.

### 12.21 Manage crawl space water
- **What:** a way for water to leave the crawl space, and every pipe that must run beside the foundation laid before backfill.
- **How:** 1) Find the low point of the crawl space floor. 2) Give it an outlet: a drain through or under the footing to daylight where the lot allows, or a sump basin for a pump where it does not. 3) Lay the downspout tightlines (solid pipe) along the foundation to their discharge point, separate from the footing drain. 4) Confirm the footing drain outfall is open and protected. 5) Photograph everything with a tape before cover.
- **Options:** gravity drain to daylight (no moving parts; needs fall); sump and pump (works on flat lots; needs power and upkeep); neither, where the plans and soil show a dry site.
- **Recommendation:** gravity wherever the lot gives you fall. Set the basin now even if you never fit a pump; it cannot be added cheaply later [E].
- **Standard:** discharge goes where the approved drainage plan says, not to the septic field or a neighbor [E — confirm against your permit conditions]. Roof water and footing drains stay in separate pipes until the discharge point (12.20).
- **Why now:** final grading and downspout connections happen in 18.2, but trenching against a finished, backfilled foundation then is slow and risks the drain and dampproofing.
- **Weather:** the crawl space is an open pond until the roof is on. Keep the ground cover out until dry-in (13.19) and pump standing water rather than leaving it against the footings.
- **Done when:** water poured at the high end of the crawl space reaches the outlet, and every tightline is in, capped above grade, and photographed.

### 12.22 Prepare the crawl space
- **What:** pier pads complete, debris and form lumber removed, ground graded to drain, vapor barrier staged.
- **Standard:** wood closer to ground than code clearances (joists 18 in., beams 12 in.) must be treated or decay-resistant [V].
- **Do now for later:** remove all wood scraps; they attract insects. Confirm crawl access is usable for the plumber and inspector.
- **Done when:** crawl space is clean and drains to its low point.

### 12.23 Backfill and rough grade
- **What:** soil placed against the walls in lifts and graded to drain away.
- **How:** backfill both sides evenly in lifts; keep heavy equipment back from the wall; slope finished rough grade away.
- **Standard:** fall of 6 in. in the first 10 ft [V]. Walls retaining more than 48 in. of unbalanced fill without top support need engineered design [V].
- **Weather and safety:** a wall holding back 4 ft or more of fill on one side is not backfilled until the floor framing is anchored to it or it is braced, and the concrete has gained strength [E]. Place fill in lifts of about 8 to 12 in., use only hand-operated compactors within about 3 ft of the wall, and keep excavators and loaded trucks back from the wall by at least the height of the fill [E]. Keep frozen lumps, wood, and rocks over about 6 in. out of the fill against the wall [E].
- **Done when:** grade slopes away on all sides and no wall shows movement or cracking.

### 12.24 Pour the garage slab, if the house has an attached garage
- **What:** the garage floor slab. The assumed build has no attached garage; skip this step if yours has none.
- **How:** 1) Compact the base in lifts and place the rock base the plans call for. 2) Install any under-slab piping or conduit and have it inspected. 3) Place the vapor barrier and reinforcement where the plans show them. 4) Set isolation joint material against the stem walls and any posts. 5) Set screeds so the slab slopes to the vehicle door. 6) Pour, finish, cut control joints, and cure.
- **Options:** pour now (truck and pump access is easy; slab is a clean work surface for framing); pour after dry-in (protected from rain and from framing damage; harder access). Hire a flatwork crew, or place and finish it yourself.
- **Recommendation:** hire the finishing. A slab is finished in one short window and a poor finish is permanent [E].
- **Standard:** thickness, reinforcement, and vapor barrier per the plans. The model code requires garage floors to slope toward the vehicle door or a drain [E — confirm against your plans].
- **Inspector checks:** under-slab plumbing or mechanical before cover, and an under-slab inspection where the slab has in-slab piping, insulation, or a vapor barrier [E — ask the county which apply to your permit].
- **Track:** T2 contractor rules in Module 2 apply to self-performed work.
- **Done when:** slab is poured, jointed, curing, and protected from traffic for the period the supplier gives.
- **If it goes wrong:** slab poured over uncompacted fill beside the stem wall settles and cracks. Compact in thin lifts or use rock.

### 12.25 Close out the module
- **What:** binder updated, bills paid, schedule and budget reset.
- **How:** 1) File inspection approvals, as-built, embed photos, concrete delivery tickets. 2) Collect lien waivers from any hired trade and supplier. 3) Update cost-to-complete. 4) Request the lender draw if one is tied to foundation. 5) Confirm lumber and framing dates.
- **Done when:** the module gate below is fully checked.
