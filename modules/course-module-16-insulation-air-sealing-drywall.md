# Module 16 — Insulation, Air Sealing, and Drywall

**Draft v1 — 15 Nov 2026 — not yet reviewed by an energy rater, builder, or inspector. Do not build from this draft.** [V] verified against a cited checklist, [E] trade practice. T1 owner-occupied, T2 spec.

**Outcome:** when you finish this module the house will be air-sealed and insulated to the energy code and the credits you claimed, inspected, and closed in with finished drywall ready for paint.

**Before you start:** framing inspection approved (15.16); energy worksheet and credit list (4.10); wall photos complete (15.13); framing dry.

**Why order matters:** air sealing is nearly free before insulation and nearly impossible after drywall. The blower door test at the end (19.2) grades this module.

---

### 16.1 Plan the air barrier
- **What:** a marked-up section drawing showing one continuous air barrier line around the heated space.
- **How:** trace it with a red pen without lifting: subfloor, rim joist, exterior wall sheathing or drywall, top plates, ceiling drywall. Circle every place the line crosses a joint or penetration.
- **Standard:** the target is the leakage rate in your energy compliance. Under the 2021 state energy code now in force, no house may test above 4.0 air changes per hour at 50 pascals on any compliance path [V]. The county's final checklist still prints the 2018-code figure of 5; do not build to it. Air-leakage credits set lower targets of 2.0, 1.5, or 0.6, and each of those credits also requires a heat recovery ventilator shown on the permit drawings [V]. Use the number on your approved energy form.
- **Output:** air barrier drawing and target number.

### 16.2 Air-seal
- **What:** every joint and hole in the air barrier sealed.
- **Checklist [V for the first three, E for the rest]:**
    - Bottom plates and inside exterior wall corners caulked.
    - Joints around windows, doors, and all utility penetrations sealed or gasketed.
    - Recessed lights rated for insulation contact, or enclosed in a sealed assembly.
    - Top plates: every wire and pipe hole foamed; plate-to-drywall joint gasketed or caulked.
    - Rim joist sealed at the sill and subfloor.
    - Electrical boxes on exterior walls and ceilings sealed.
    - Tub and shower surrounds on exterior walls backed with an air barrier before the tub goes in (check 15.3).
    - Attic hatch weather-stripped (16.6).
    - Duct boots sealed to the subfloor or drywall.
- **Crew, time, tools:** 2 people, 2–3 days; sealant, foam gun, gaskets, flashlight.
- **Done when:** you have checked every circled item on the drawing.

### 16.3 Pre-test for leaks
- **What:** leaks found and fixed while the walls are open.
- **Options:** hire an energy rater for a pre-drywall blower door test (best; the same person can do the final); rent or borrow a fan and use a smoke pencil; or inspect by eye with a strong light on a windy day.
- **Recommendation:** pay for the pre-test if you claimed an air-leakage credit. One visit costs far less than failing at final.
- **Note:** a pre-drywall test only works where the air barrier is at the sheathing or a membrane. If the ceiling drywall is your air barrier, test after hanging and before finishing.

### 16.4 Insulate walls
- **What:** wall cavities filled, plus the continuous exterior insulation if your compliance path uses it.
- **Standard:** walls above grade R-20 plus R-5 continuous, or R-13 plus R-10 continuous, under the 2021 prescriptive table; R-value marking visible to the inspector; faced batts stapled over the framing face, or unfaced batts with a wall vapor retarder or a vapor-retarder primer on the drywall; vapor retarder rated 1 perm or less [V].
- **How:** fill each cavity completely; split batts around wires; cut to fit around boxes; no compression, no gaps.
- **Options [E]:** batts (owner-friendly; quality depends on patience); blown-in dense fill behind netting (better fill, usually hired); spray foam (hired; installer certifies thickness and R-value [V]).
- **Note:** if your plans use continuous exterior insulation, it was installed with the envelope in Module 14. Check the plans.

### 16.5 Insulate the floor and finish the crawl space
- **What:** floor insulation supported in contact with the subfloor, and a ground cover over the crawl space soil.
- **Standard:** floors R-30; insulation in substantial contact with the subfloor; supports no more than 24 in. apart; insulation not blocking foundation vents; rim joist insulated [V]. Ground cover of black polyethylene, run to the foundation walls: the state residential code amendment now in force requires at least 6 mil, lapped at least 6 in. at joints [V]. The 10 mil with 12-in. laps in the regional 2021 checklist is the practice of the cities that publish it, not the state minimum [V]; heavier plastic survives foot traffic better and is a reasonable upgrade [E].
- **Inspector checks:** the crawl space is checked again at final [V].
- **Later trades:** remove all debris; keep the underfloor cleanout and access clear.

### 16.6 Prepare the attic
- **What:** baffles at every eave vent, depth markers, a dam and gasket at the hatch, and all ceiling penetrations sealed from above.
- **Standard:** baffles keep at least 1 in. clear below the roof sheathing; depth markers at least one per 300 sq ft facing the access; hatch insulated to match, weather-stripped, with a retaining curb as high as the insulation [V].
- **Do now for later:** the loose-fill insulation itself is blown after drywall (16.11).

### 16.7 Pass insulation inspection (wait)
- **What:** approval to cover.
- **How:** request after all concealed areas (walls, any vaulted ceilings) are insulated and before anything is covered. The county inspects accessible attic and underfloor insulation at the final inspection, and may require a separate inspection of exterior rigid insulation where the energy form uses it [V]. Have baffles and depth markers in place.
- **Inspector checks:** R-values, fit, vapor retarder, air sealing items, baffles [V].

### 16.8 Hang drywall
- **What:** board hung on ceilings first, then walls, fastened to the schedule.
- **How:** ceilings perpendicular to framing. With trusses at 24 in. on center, use 5/8 in. board or 1/2 in. board made as ceiling board, not standard 1/2 in. wallboard: the gypsum industry standard requires that where a water-based texture is applied [V], and R-60 loose fill adds load on top [E]. Check the board maker's limit for insulation weight before choosing cellulose. Walls horizontal; stagger joints; keep joints off door and window corners; cut boxes tight.
- **Standard:** where there is an attached garage, at least 1/2 in. gypsum on the garage side of the wall to the house and 5/8 in. Type X on a garage ceiling under habitable rooms [V]. Moisture-resistant or cement board where the plans call for it in wet areas.
- **Options:** hang it yourselves (rent a lift for ceilings; heavy but learnable); hire hanging (fast; often done in a day or two).
- **Crew, time, tools:** 2–3 people; drywall lift; screw gun. Have the board delivered by boom truck after the insulation inspection and spread it across rooms, not stacked at midspan [E].
- **Point of no return:** everything behind the board is now hidden. Check the photo set is complete first.

### 16.9 Pass drywall fastening inspection where required (wait)
- **What:** approval of fastening before tape, if your jurisdiction requires it (commonly for rated assemblies and shear-rated gypsum).
- **How:** the county lists a Gypsum Board/Wall Assembly inspection, requested after the board is hung and before joints and fasteners are taped. It covers only garage-to-dwelling separation walls and ceilings, 5/8 in. Type X board required for fire flow, gypsum used as braced wall panels, and other rated assemblies on the approved plans [V]. A house with an attached garage needs it; request it for those areas before the tapers start.

### 16.10 Tape and finish
- **What:** joints taped and finished to the level your paint needs.
- **Options:** hire the finishing (strongly recommended; it is a skill that takes years and is on view forever); finish it yourselves (budget three to four times the hours you expect).
- **Standard:** finish level and texture agreed in writing with the finisher; corners straight; no ridges under raking light [E].
- **Weather:** compound needs warmth and air movement to dry. Provide temporary heat and dehumidification; avoid unvented fuel heaters that add moisture.

### 16.11 Insulate the attic
- **What:** loose-fill insulation blown to the marked depth.
- **Standard:** ceilings R-60 under the 2021 prescriptive table; insulation kept out of baffles and clear of any fuel-burning vents [V].
- **Options:** rent a blower from the supplier (two people, one day); hire it.
- **Done when:** every depth marker reads at or above the required depth.

### 16.12 Prime, dry out, close out
- **What:** drywall primed, house drying, records filed.
- **Standard:** if unfaced batts were used without a separate vapor retarder, the primer must be the vapor-retarder type [V].
- **Output:** insulation certificate details for the energy certificate (19.3); cost and schedule updated.

## Top mistakes
1. Insulating before air sealing (16.2).
2. Compressed or gapped batts (16.4).
3. Failing the blower door test at final with no way to reach the leaks (16.3).
4. Finishing drywall in a cold, wet house (16.10).
5. Blocking eave vents with insulation (16.6).

## Confirm with a professional
- The air-leakage target and test method on your approved energy form.
- Ground cover thickness the inspector will accept.
- Whether a drywall fastening inspection applies.
- Vapor retarder approach for your wall assembly, especially with exterior continuous insulation.
