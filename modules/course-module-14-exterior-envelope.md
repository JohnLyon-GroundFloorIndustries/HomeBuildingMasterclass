# Module 14 — Exterior Envelope

**Draft v1.1 — 15 Nov 2026 — not yet reviewed by a builder or inspector. Do not build from this draft.** [V] verified against a cited checklist, [E] trade practice. Manufacturer instructions govern every product named here. T1 owner-occupied, T2 spec.

**Assumed build:** wood sheathing, mechanically attached house wrap, flanged windows, fiber-cement lap siding over a rainscreen gap, marine climate.

**Outcome:** when you finish this module the walls will shed water and drain what gets behind the siding, with every opening and penetration flashed in the right order.

**Before you start:** sheathing and shear inspection approved (13.18); roof dried in; windows and doors on site; siding and trim on site or scheduled.

**The one rule:** every layer laps over the layer below it, like shingles. Water moving down the wall must always land on the outside of the next piece. Sealant is the backup, never the plan.

**Inspection note:** the county inspects sheathing and shear before wrap or flashing goes on [V]. Ask at that inspection whether your jurisdiction wants to see window flashing or the wrap before siding. The county expects windows and exterior siding to be in place at the framing inspection (15.16), so do not postpone siding until after framing approval [V].

**Variant — continuous exterior insulation.** The 2021 prescriptive wall is R-20 in the cavity plus R-5 continuous, or R-13 plus R-10 continuous [V]. If your approved energy form uses that path instead of a trade-off, rigid insulation goes on the outside of the sheathing in this module, and it changes four steps:

- 14.2: decide with your designer whether the wrap goes under or over the foam; the window detail follows from that choice.
- 14.3: windows need extended bucks or a manufacturer-approved method for mounting over foam.
- 14.6: furring strips fastened through the foam into the studs create the rainscreen gap and carry the siding.
- 14.7: longer fasteners, per the siding and foam manufacturers' tables.

Confirm which wall you are building before ordering windows (9.6). This variant is not detailed further in this draft.

### 14.1 Draw every water-management detail
- **What:** a hand sketch, in section, of each condition on your house: window sill, jamb, and head; door sill; wall bottom at the foundation; roof-to-wall; deck ledger; hose bib; vent hood; light block; meter base; inside and outside corners.
- **How:** number the layers in installation order on each sketch. If you cannot number them so each laps the one below, the detail is wrong.
- **Standard:** the window, wrap, and siding manufacturers' instructions agree with your sketch.
- **Crew, time:** 1 person, an evening.
- **Output:** detail sheet, in the binder and taped up on site.

### 14.2 Install the water-resistive barrier
- **What:** house wrap over all wall sheathing.
- **How:** start at the bottom; lap upper courses over lower; lap vertical seams; tape seams if the product requires it; fasten with cap fasteners; lap over the foundation flashing at the bottom.
- **Standard:** horizontal laps at least 6 in. and vertical laps at least 6–12 in. unless the manufacturer says otherwise [E].
- **Options:** mechanically attached wrap (cheapest, familiar); self-adhered membrane (better air barrier, more cost, unforgiving); sheathing with an integrated barrier and taped seams (fast, chosen at 13.2, not now).
- **Later trades:** if the wrap is also your air barrier for an energy credit, seams and edges must be taped and sealed to the foundation and top plate (16.1).
- **Weather:** wrap degrades in sunlight. Check the product's exposure limit and side within it.

### 14.3 Flash openings and install windows
- **What:** every window installed plumb, level, square, flashed, and operating.
- **How (flanged window, common sequence) [E]:**
    1. Cut the wrap at the opening per the manufacturer; fold the top flap up and tape it out of the way.
    2. Sill pan: sloped sill or back dam, then flexible flashing across the sill and 6 in. up each jamb.
    3. Bead of sealant behind the side and top flanges, none across the bottom so water can drain.
    4. Set the window; shim; check diagonals; fasten per instructions.
    5. Jamb flashing tape over the side flanges.
    6. Head flashing tape over the top flange, then a rigid drip cap where required.
    7. Fold the wrap flap down over the head flashing and tape the diagonal cuts. Where the head trim will carry a metal Z-flashing (14.7), its upper leg must also end up behind the wrap: leave the flap loose until trim and flashing are on, or slit the wrap above the trim, slip the leg in, and tape the slit. With furring strips, hold them back so the flashing leg sits against the wrap [E].
- **Standard:** bedroom windows meet emergency escape rules: sill no more than 44 in. above the floor; clear opening at least 5.7 sq ft (5.0 at grade floor), at least 20 in. wide and 24 in. high [V]. Safety glazing where required, including near doors, tubs, showers, stairs, and large low panes [V].
- **Inspector checks:** egress sizes and safety glazing marks at framing and final [V].
- **Crew, time:** 2 people, 20–40 minutes per window once practiced.
- **If it goes wrong:** a window is out of square and binds. Fix it before the flashing tape goes on.

### 14.4 Install exterior doors
- **What:** doors set on a sill pan, plumb, with even reveals.
- **How:** sill pan or membrane with a back dam; sealant under the threshold; shim at hinges and strike; flash jambs and head as for windows.
- **Standard:** the door between an attached garage and the house is solid wood or steel at least 1-3/8 in. thick or 20-minute rated, self-closing, and weather-stripped [V].

### 14.5 Flash penetrations
- **What:** mounting blocks and flashing at every pipe, vent, wire, light, outlet, hose bib, and meter base.
- **How:** install a block or flanged boot; flash it into the wrap like a small window: bottom open to drain, sides, then top under the wrap above.
- **Later trades:** walk the walls with the electrical and mechanical plans first (15.1). Every exterior light, outlet, exhaust hood, heat pump line set, and hose bib needs its block before siding.
- **Standard:** hose bibs are frost-proof with vacuum breakers and sealed at the wall [V].

### 14.6 Install the rainscreen gap
- **What:** vertical furring strips or a drainage mat creating a gap behind the siding, with insect screen at top and bottom.
- **Standard:** a gap of about 3/8 in. is typical in this climate [E]. Confirm whether your plans or siding manufacturer require it.
- **Options:** furring strips (cheap, slower); drainable wrap or mat (fast, more material cost); none (allowed by some products, least forgiving).
- **Recommendation:** install the gap. In a wet climate it is the cheapest insurance in the house.

### 14.7 Install trim and siding
- **What:** corner boards, window and door trim, and siding installed to the manufacturer's instructions.
- **Standard:** siding at least 6 in. above ground and 2 in. above concrete [V]. Gaps at trim and butt joints, fastener type and placement, and clearances above roofs and flashings per the manufacturer.
- **How:** set a level starting line around the house; use a story pole so courses align at corners and openings; flash butt joints as required; prime or seal cut ends.
- **Safety:** cutting fiber cement makes hazardous dust. Use shears or a saw with dust collection, outdoors, with a respirator.
- **Crew, time:** 2 people, 2–4 weeks part-time [E].

### 14.8 Build decks and porches
- **What:** any attached deck or porch framed, flashed, and connected.
- **Standard:** placement and size per plans; designed for 60 psf live load; treated or naturally durable wood with cut ends treated; corrosion-resistant fasteners; ledger attached per the code table; lateral connection by two 1,500-lb devices or four 750-lb devices unless the deck is under 30 in. above grade; footings at least 12 in. below grade; guards where the walking surface is more than 30 in. above grade [V].
- **How:** flash the ledger: membrane on the wall, ledger attached as detailed, metal flashing over the ledger top tucked under the wrap.
- **If it goes wrong:** the ledger is nailed, not bolted or screwed with structural fasteners. That is the classic deck failure. The band joist behind the ledger must be 2 in. nominal lumber or 1 in. engineered rim board bearing fully on the wall or foundation below [V]. Do not hang a ledger over siding, on brick or stone veneer, or on a cantilevered floor without the engineer's detail [E]. Kitsap does not accept precast pier blocks as deck footings and expects lateral hold-downs in at least two locations on each section of a deck over 30 in. high [V]. A low deck left unattached to the house still needs its own bracing [E].

### 14.9 Install soffits, fascia, and gutters
- **What:** vented soffits, gutters, and downspouts connected to the approved drainage. Fascia and rake boards went on before the drip edge (13.19); here they get final caulk and paint. Install soffits before the top courses of siding and the frieze trim in 14.7, which finish against them [E].
- **Standard:** soffit vents stay clear so attic ventilation works (16.6). Eaves must not project into required fire separation distances near property lines [V].
- **Later trades:** downspout discharge is part of the stormwater plan (18.2).

### 14.10 Caulk and paint
- **What:** sealant at trim joints and penetrations, then finish coats.
- **Standard:** windows, penetrations, and openings are sealed [V]. Do not caulk the bottom edge of window trim or the bottom of siding laps; water needs a way out [E].
- **Weather:** paint and sealant have temperature and moisture limits printed on the can.

### 14.11 Water-test
- **What:** a hose test of each window, door, and tricky flashing before interior finishes.
- **How:** one person sprays from the bottom up for several minutes per opening; another watches inside with a light.
- **Standard:** no water inside. Fix now, while the framing is still open.

### 14.12 Close out
- **What:** photos of every flashing detail before siding, product data and warranties filed, cost and schedule updated.

## Top mistakes
1. Reverse laps at window heads (14.3).
2. Sealing the bottom flange or bottom trim edge (14.3, 14.10).
3. Penetrations cut through finished siding with no block or flashing (14.5).
4. Siding too close to the ground or roof (14.7).
5. Unflashed, under-fastened deck ledger (14.8).
6. Ordering windows before deciding whether the wall has exterior foam (variant).

## Confirm with a professional
- Whether your energy compliance requires continuous exterior insulation, and the full detail set if it does.
- Whether the jurisdiction inspects wrap or window flashing before siding.
- Rainscreen requirement for the chosen siding.
- Lap dimensions and exposure limits for the chosen wrap.
- Fire separation rules if any wall is near a property line.
