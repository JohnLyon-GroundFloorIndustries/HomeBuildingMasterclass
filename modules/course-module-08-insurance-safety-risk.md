# Module 8 — Insurance, Safety, and Risk

**Draft v1 — 15 Nov 2026 — not insurance or legal advice; not yet reviewed by a broker.** [V] verified, [E] estimate. T1 owner-occupied, T2 spec.

**Outcome:** when you finish this module you will have coverage in force for the structure, for liability, and for anyone who works on the site, plus a written safety plan and a risk register.

**Before you start:** legal scope matrix and helper policy (Module 2), budget and schedule drafts, lender requirements if any.

---

### 8.1 Meet an insurance broker
- **What:** written quotes and a coverage summary for your project.
- **How:** use an independent broker who places construction policies. Bring the budget, schedule, and the list of who will work on site.
- **Ask:** "What covers the structure and materials during construction, including theft and materials stored off site? What covers injury to a visitor, a delivery driver, a friend helping, a paid helper? What does my existing homeowner or umbrella policy exclude? What does the lender require?"
- **Standard:** a homeowner policy on your current home generally does not cover a house under construction elsewhere [E].

### 8.2 Bind builder's risk
- **What:** a builder's risk (course of construction) policy in force before materials arrive.
- **Standard:** limit equal to the completed value excluding land; term covering the owner-pace schedule with extensions available; theft of materials included [E].
- **Done when:** you hold the declarations page and the lender is named if required.
- **If it goes wrong:** the policy expires before the house is done. Calendar the expiry 60 days ahead.

### 8.3 Bind liability coverage
- **What:** liability coverage for the site.
- **Options [E]:** an endorsement or separate policy for the vacant land and construction site; an umbrella policy; for T2 under a contractor registration, the general liability policy required for registration.
- **Standard:** registered contractors must carry at least $200,000 public liability and $50,000 property damage, or $250,000 combined [V]. That is a legal minimum, not a recommendation.
- **Track:** T2 should ask about completed-operations coverage that continues after the sale.

### 8.4 Set the rule for trade insurance
- **What:** a rule that no trade starts without proof of registration and insurance.
- **How:** 1) Look up each contractor on the state Verify tool. 2) Get a certificate of insurance sent directly from their agent. 3) File both.
- **Standard:** registered, bonded, insured, and with workers' compensation if they have employees. Registered contractors are required to hold a bond and liability insurance [V].
- **If it goes wrong:** a certificate has lapsed by the time they arrive. Re-check on the start date.

### 8.5 Cover helpers
- **What:** coverage or a clear rule for every non-contractor who works on site.
- **Standard:** do not assume a homeowner exemption for building a new house. The department's guidance is that an owner who directly hires workers for new construction must provide workers' compensation insurance; the statute's private-home exclusion covers only gardening, maintenance, and repair [V]. The statute also excludes employment that is outside the employer's trade or business and not at the employer's private home [V]. Whether that could reach a T1 helper on a house you do not yet live in is a question for the department in writing; it cannot apply to a T2 build, which is a business [E]. An employer may also elect coverage for excluded workers [V]. Open the account before the first paid hour.
- **How:** apply the helper policy from 2.9. Decide which tasks unpaid helpers may do; keep them off roofs, out of trenches, and away from heavy equipment.

### 8.6 Write the site safety plan
- **What:** two pages covering the hazards that kill and injure on residential sites.
- **Contents [E]:**
    1. Falls: state rules for employers require fall protection at 4 ft or more, and on roofs steeper than 4 in 12 only fall restraint, personal fall arrest, or positioning systems are accepted [V]. Hold the family to the same line: a harness, rope grab, and rated roof anchor for every person on the roof, anchors fastened into framing per their instructions, from the first sheet of sheathing; guardrails at open floor edges; ladders tied off and extended 3 ft above the landing; floor openings covered [E].
    2. Trenches and excavations: no one in a trench or cut 4 ft deep or more without sloping, shoring, or a trench shield, the depth at which state rules require cave-in protection for employees [V]; apply it shallower in loose, filled, or wet soil; spoils and equipment kept at least 2 ft back from the edge; never work alone in a trench [E].
    3. Equipment: one operator, a spotter, no riders, no one under a raised load.
    4. Power tools: guards on, eye and hearing protection, no cords in water. Framing nailers set to sequential (single-shot) trigger, not bump-fire; free hand kept out of the line of the nail; air disconnected before clearing a jam [E].
    5. Lifting: trusses and walls raised with a plan and enough people or a crane.
    6. Silica and dust: wet cutting or vacuum for concrete and fiber cement.
    7. First aid kit, fire extinguisher, charged phone, site address posted for emergency responders.
    8. Children on site: only with a dedicated adult, never during lifts, pours, or roofing.
- **Standard:** if you employ anyone, state workplace safety rules apply to you as an employer [E].
- **Done when:** everyone who works on site has read and signed it.

### 8.7 Secure the site
- **What:** fencing or barriers where needed, locked storage, signage, and lighting.
- **How:** a locked container for tools; deliveries timed to installation so materials do not sit; cameras where power allows.
- **Standard:** copper, tools, appliances, and windows are the usual theft targets [E].

### 8.8 Build the risk register
- **What:** a table of risks with likelihood, cost, prevention, and response.
- **Starting list:** owner injury or unavailability; trade no-show; failed inspection; weather before dry-in; price increases; theft; a neighbor dispute; lender delay; a hidden site condition; T2 market drop.
- **How:** for the top five, write the specific action that reduces it and the trigger for the fallback plan.
- **Standard:** review monthly with the schedule.

### 8.9 Set the incident procedure
- **What:** one page: what to do after an injury, property damage, or theft.
- **How:** emergency numbers and nearest hospital; who calls the broker; photos and written notes the same day; when to stop work.

## Top mistakes
1. Relying on a homeowner policy (8.1).
2. Paying a helper without coverage (8.5).
3. Roof and truss work without fall protection (8.6).
4. Letting builder's risk lapse on a slow build (8.2).
