# Module 17 — Interior Finishes

**Draft v1 — 15 Nov 2026 — not yet reviewed by a builder or inspector.** [V] verified against a cited checklist, [E] trade practice. Manufacturer instructions govern every product. T1 owner-occupied, T2 spec.

**Outcome:** when you finish this module the interior will be complete: painted, cabinets and counters in, tile and flooring down, doors and trim hung, and every fixture, device, and appliance installed and working.

**Before you start:** drywall primed and dry (16.12); heat running; finish materials on site or scheduled (9.6); wall photos to hand (15.13).

**Where owner labor pays most:** paint, trim, flooring, and tile are the scopes where patient amateurs do professional-quality work. This is also where projects stall, because nothing forces a deadline. Keep the weekly look-ahead running.

**Track note:** on T2, electrical and plumbing trim are licensed work; the rest falls under the contractor rules in Module 2.

---

### 17.1 Lock the finish schedule and orders
- **What:** a room-by-room schedule: paint colors, flooring, tile, cabinets, counters, doors, trim profiles, hardware, plumbing fixtures, lights, appliances, each with model, quantity, and delivery date.
- **Standard:** every appliance and fixture has its installation sheet printed; rough openings and connections were set from these in Module 15.
- **Track:** T2 selects for the buyer pool: neutral colors, durable mid-grade materials, nothing that needs explaining.
- **If it goes wrong:** a selection changes after cabinets are ordered. Treat it as a change order with cost and time.

### 17.2 Paint first coats
- **What:** ceilings finished; walls with a first finish coat.
- **How:** spray or roll before cabinets, trim, and flooring go in. Mask windows and tubs.
- **Standard:** primer type from 16.12; two finish coats total, the last after trim (17.8).
- **Crew, time:** 2 people, 3–5 days for a 2,000 sq ft house [E].

### 17.3 Install cabinets
- **What:** cabinets set level, plumb, and fastened to backing.
- **How:** 1) Find the high point of the floor. 2) Mark a level line for base cabinet tops and upper cabinet bottoms. 3) Hang uppers first from a temporary ledger. 4) Set bases, shimming to the line. 5) Screw to the backing installed in 13.21 and to each other. 6) Scribe fillers to walls.
- **Standard:** level within 1/16 in. across a run; faces flush (craft) [E]. Countertop fabricators will refuse to template out-of-level cabinets.
- **Later trades:** leave appliance openings at the sizes on the installation sheets; confirm the dishwasher, range, and refrigerator openings with a tape. Flooring follows cabinets (17.6), so wherever the finished floor is thicker than about 1/4 in., set base cabinets on plywood strips of the same thickness, or run the flooring through the dishwasher and range openings first. Check the dishwasher height against the opening measured from the finished floor, not the subfloor [E].

### 17.4 Template and install countertops (wait)
- **What:** fabricated tops installed, with sink and cooktop cutouts.
- **How:** fabricator templates after bases are fixed; sink and faucet must be on site for the template; installation typically follows in two to three weeks [E].
- **Options:** stone or solid surface (hired fabrication and installation); laminate or butcher block (owner-installable).
- **Standard:** at least 30 in. between the cooktop and unprotected combustibles above, or the reduced clearance your hood and range allow [V].

### 17.5 Waterproof and tile wet areas
- **What:** showers and tub surrounds waterproofed, tested, and tiled; other tile floors and backsplashes set and grouted.
- **How (shower) [E]:** build the slope under the waterproofing, not on top of it: a liner laid flat on the subfloor holds water in the mortar bed permanently. Install the waterproofing system exactly as its maker instructs, including corners, niches, and the drain connection; on a lined pan keep the drain's weep holes clear and put no fasteners through the curb top or inside face. Flood-test: plug the drain below the weep holes with a test plug, fill to just below the curb top, mark the water line, and wait 24 hours. Then tile. For a first shower, a bonded sheet or liquid membrane system with its own drain and preformed corners is more forgiving than a lined mortar pan.
- **Standard:** for a lined shower pan, the liner slopes at least 1/4 in. per foot to the drain, extends at least 3 in. above the finished curb, and has no fasteners lower than 1 in. above the finished curb; curb height 2–9 in.; permanent seats sloped to drain [V].
- **Inspector checks:** shower pan; fixtures sealed watertight at final [V].
- **Point of no return:** tile over a membrane that was not flood-tested.
- **If it goes wrong:** the flood test drops. Find and fix the leak before any tile is set.

### 17.6 Install hard flooring
- **What:** wood, laminate, or vinyl plank flooring installed over a flat, dry subfloor.
- **How:** acclimate material per the manufacturer; check subfloor flatness and moisture; undercut door jambs; leave the expansion gap; run under appliances as the cabinet layout requires.
- **Standard:** flatness and moisture limits are printed in the installation guide and are warranty conditions [E].
- **Weather:** the house must be at living temperature and humidity. Flooring laid in a cold, damp house will move.

### 17.7 Hang doors and install trim
- **What:** interior doors hung with even gaps; casing, baseboard, window stools, and closet shelving installed; stairs and rails complete.
- **Standard:** stair and rail rules: risers no more than 7-3/4 in. and treads at least 10 in., varying no more than 3/8 in.; handrails on stairs with four or more risers, 34–38 in. high, graspable, returned to the wall; guards at least 36 in. high where the drop exceeds 30 in., with openings that do not pass a 4-in. sphere [V].
- **How:** shim hinges first, then strike side; nail trim into framing, not just drywall.

### 17.8 Final paint
- **What:** trim painted, walls second-coated, touch-up complete.
- **Standard:** caulk trim joints before paint; one person inspects under raking light.

### 17.9 Electrical trim
- **What:** devices, cover plates, lights, fans, and alarms installed; panel labeled; power on.
- **Standard:** smoke alarms on every floor, in each bedroom, and in halls serving bedrooms, interconnected; carbon monoxide alarms on every floor and near sleeping areas [V]. Protection devices and labeling per the electrical code [E].
- **How:** test every outlet, switch, and alarm; fill in the panel directory legibly.

### 17.10 Plumbing trim
- **What:** toilets, sinks, faucets, shower trim, and water heater installed and leak-tested.
- **Standard (water heater):** temperature and pressure relief valve installed per its listing; relief drain in full-size rigid pipe, running by gravity with no trap, discharging outside the building 6–24 in. above the ground and pointing down, or through an air gap into the drainage system, never into the heater pan, with the end unthreaded and visible [V]; the state also requires a consumer electric storage water heater to have a demand-response communications port [V]; two seismic straps, in the upper and lower thirds, fastened to studs; a pan with drain where the heater sits in an attic or floor assembly; an expansion tank where a check valve or backflow device closes the system [V].
- **Standard (fixtures):** shower and tub water limited to 120°F; hot water delivered at 100°F or more; fixtures sealed at walls and floors; all fixtures run and checked for leaks; hose bibs with vacuum breakers [V].
- **Heat pump water heater:** an all-electric house will likely use one. It needs a condensate drain, the room air volume or ducting its maker requires, and service clearance. Plan these at rough-in (15.5) [E].
- **How:** fill and run every fixture; check each trap and supply under load; look again the next day.

### 17.11 Heating and cooling trim and start-up
- **What:** registers and grilles, thermostat, outdoor unit set and connected, system charged, started, and balanced.
- **Standard:** outdoor equipment pad at least 3 in. above grade; condensate drained properly; ventilation set to run as designed [V].
- **Ventilation check:** state code requires whole-house and local exhaust airflows to be measured and verified, and whole-house ventilation to run continuously unless it has intermittent controls sized for that [V].
- **Who:** a certified technician handles refrigerant and start-up [E]. Keep the start-up report; it supports the warranty and the energy certificate.

### 17.12 Install appliances
- **What:** range, hood, dishwasher, refrigerator, washer, dryer installed to their sheets.
- **Standard:** dryer connected with a metal transition duct no longer than 8 ft, not passing through walls or floors; clearances to combustibles per the manufacturer [V].
- **How:** install anti-tip brackets and any manufacturer-required restraints; run a cycle on each.

### 17.13 Carpet, hardware, and accessories
- **What:** carpet laid; door hardware, cabinet pulls, mirrors, towel bars, closet rods, and shower doors installed.
- **How:** use the backing from 13.21 and the wall photos from 15.13 to find it.

### 17.14 Walk the pre-punch list
- **What:** a written list of every defect, room by room, before any inspector or buyer sees the house.
- **How:** one person with blue tape and a notepad; operate every door, window, drawer, fixture, switch, and outlet; look under raking light; check every caulk joint.
- **Done when:** the list is complete and each item has an owner and a date.

## Top mistakes
1. Tiling over untested waterproofing (17.5).
2. Out-of-level cabinets (17.3).
3. Flooring installed in a cold or damp house (17.6).
4. Finish selections changing after orders (17.1).
5. Losing momentum; the finish phase doubling in length.

## Confirm with a professional
- Shower waterproofing method and whether the jurisdiction inspects the pan.
- Electrical protection and labeling requirements at trim (electrician or state inspector).
- Heat pump start-up requirements and who may perform them.
