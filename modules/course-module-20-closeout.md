# Module 20 — Closeout: Move In (T1) or Sell (T2)

**Draft v1 — 15 Nov 2026 — not legal, tax, or real estate advice; not yet reviewed by an attorney, CPA, or agent.** [V] verified, [E] estimate or general practice.

**Outcome:** when you finish this module the project will be financially and legally closed: either you are living in the house with permanent financing and a maintenance plan, or the house is sold, the loan is paid off, and you know what you earned.

**Before you start:** certificate of occupancy and closeout binder (Module 19), final cost report, loan documents, the occupancy statement or contractor path from Module 2.

Follow steps 20.1–20.6 for an owner-occupied build, or 20.7–20.15 for a spec build.

---

## Track 1 — Move in

### 20.1 Final draw and loan conversion [T1]
- **What:** last construction draw paid and the loan converted to permanent financing, or the refinance closed.
- **How:** send the lender the certificate of occupancy and final lien waivers; schedule their final inspection; confirm the conversion date and first payment.
- **Standard:** convert before the construction loan's maturity date (7.10).
- **If it goes wrong:** a rate lock expires during a slow finish. Ask about extension cost well before it lapses.

### 20.2 Switch to homeowner insurance [T1]
- **What:** homeowner policy in force from the day you occupy; builder's risk cancelled.
- **How:** call the broker a week ahead. Occupancy can end builder's risk coverage automatically [E].

### 20.3 Set the maintenance schedule [T1]
- **What:** a calendar of recurring maintenance.
- **Items:** septic inspection every three years by a certified provider [V]; gutter cleaning; heat pump filters and annual service; ventilation filters; crawl space check each fall and spring; caulk and paint inspection each year; water heater flush; smoke and carbon monoxide alarm tests; well water test if on a well.
- **Standard:** keep vehicles, structures, and deep-rooted plants off the drainfield and reserve area permanently.

### 20.4 File warranties and manuals [T1]
- **What:** product registrations submitted; warranties and manuals filed in the binder.
- **How:** register roofing, windows, heat pump, water heater, and appliances; note each warranty's conditions, such as required professional start-up or annual service.

### 20.5 Calendar your occupancy commitments [T1]
- **What:** the dates before which a sale would conflict with what you signed.
- **Standard:** the owner electrical permit for a new home required living there at least 24 months after finishing the electrical work [V]. For contractor registration, a person who offers a house for sale without having occupied it for more than one year after substantial completion is defined as a contractor, and the exemption for working on your own property does not cover work done for the purpose of selling, whatever the date [V].
- **How:** put both dates on the calendar: 24 months after the electrical work was completed, and one year of occupancy after the house was substantially complete. Add a third date: two years after the day you move in, the earliest sale date at which the full federal home-sale exclusion can apply [V]. If your plans change before any of these, call the attorney and the CPA before listing. A job move of 50 miles or more, including military orders, can preserve a prorated part of the exclusion; it does not change the state licensing dates [V for the tax rule]. Whenever you do sell, you will complete the full seller disclosure statement, including the structural and systems sections, from your actual knowledge as the person who built the house [V].

### 20.6 Collect final waivers and close accounts [T1]
- **What:** final unconditional lien waivers from every trade and supplier; project bank account reconciled; records kept for your cost basis.
- **Standard:** a lien must be recorded within 90 days after the claimant's last work or delivery [V]. Do not treat the project as lien-free until that period has passed for the last supplier.
- **Who:** give the CPA the final cost report. Your basis is the cash cost of land and construction, including sales tax paid; the value of your own labor and of unpaid help is not included [V]. Keep the report, receipts, settlement statements, and permit record for as long as you own the house and at least three years after the return for the year you sell [E].
- **Expect:** the assessor values new construction as it stood on 31 July and adds it to the roll for tax due the following year [V]. A house complete by 31 July is taxed at full value the next year; one finished later is taxed on its partial value for one more year [E]. Budget the higher payment and tell the mortgage servicer so the escrow account is not short. Federal energy credits for homeowners ended for property placed in service after 2025 [V].

## Track 2 — Sell

### 20.7 Set the price [T2]
- **What:** a list price supported by comparable sales.
- **How:** pull closed sales from the last three to six months for similar new or recent homes nearby; adjust for size, lot, and finish; set the price where the house competes, then check it against your pro forma.
- **Standard:** price to the market, not to your costs. Every month unsold adds interest, insurance, taxes, and utilities.
- **If it goes wrong:** the market price is below your break-even. Decide quickly between selling at a loss, renting, or moving in, and call the CPA and lender first. Selling at a loss: a loss on a house held for sale in a business is generally an ordinary loss [E]. Renting for 30 days or more at a time: the rent is not subject to state business tax [V]; the house becomes depreciable rental property and the construction loan must be replaced [E]. Moving in: the home-sale exclusion needs two years of actual use [V], and the licensing rules for a house built for sale still governed how it was built; ask the attorney.

### 20.8 Choose the sale method [T2]
- **Options [E]:**

| Option | Cost | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| A. Full-service listing agent | Commission | Most first-time spec sellers |
| B. Flat-fee or limited-service listing | Lower fee | Sellers comfortable handling showings and negotiation |
| C. Sale by owner | Lowest cash cost | Only with attorney support and a ready buyer |
| D. Pre-sale during construction | Varies | Reduces market risk; can change your state tax treatment |

- **Standard:** if a speculative builder sells or contracts to sell during construction in a way that gives the buyer title or an immediate right to possession, construction after that date is a retail sale. The builder owes retailing business tax and must collect sales tax on the part of the price allocable to that work, and takes a deduction for sales tax already paid on the materials and contractor charges used in it [V]. A purchase agreement signed during construction that closes after completion does not transfer ownership until closing, even with a large deposit [V]. Have the CPA read the agreement before you sign; early possession is the clear trigger, and buyer-directed changes deserve a question [E].
- **Recommendation:** A for a first spec sale.

### 20.9 Prepare disclosures and warranty [T2]
- **What:** the seller disclosure documents your attorney says apply, a written builder warranty, and the buyer's copy of the binder.
- **Standard (disclosure):** new construction is not exempt from the seller disclosure statement. For new construction that has never been occupied, the seller may omit the structural section and the systems and fixtures section; the rest of the form, including title, water, sewer or septic, and environmental questions, still applies [V]. Deliver it within five business days after mutual acceptance unless otherwise agreed; the buyer then has three business days to accept it or rescind [V]. A buyer may expressly waive the statement, but not the environmental section if any answer there would be yes [V]. Answers are from your actual knowledge, and you must amend or correct if you learn before closing that an answer has become inaccurate [V]. The statutory form changes on 1 January 2027; use the version in force when you sell [V].
- **Standard (implied warranty):** Washington courts imply a warranty of habitability when a builder-vendor sells a new house to its first occupant. It reaches serious structural and foundation defects that make the house unfit to live in, not every flaw [V]. It applies to a commercial sale by someone regularly engaged in building, as opposed to a casual or personal sale, and which side of that line a first-time spec builder falls on is a question of fact [V]. Plan as if it applies to a T2 sale. A disclaimer works only if it is conspicuous, known to the buyer, and specifically bargained for [V, from a legislative summary of the case law].
- **Standard (right to cure notice):** a builder who contracts to sell a residence must give the buyer a conspicuous notice, in the statutory wording, of the builder's right to offer to cure defects before the buyer may sue. It may be part of the purchase contract [V].
- **How:** ask the attorney: whether the implied warranty applies on your facts; what a written limited warranty should say and whether any disclaimer is worth attempting; where the cure notice goes in the purchase contract; how long your exposure lasts (20.14).

### 20.10 Stage and photograph [T2]
- **What:** a clean, finished, lit house with professional photos.
- **How:** complete every punch item first; clean windows; landscape the front; stage the main rooms or leave them cleanly empty; hire a real estate photographer.

### 20.11 List and show [T2]
- **What:** the listing live, with a showing plan.
- **How:** provide the feature sheet: energy features and test results, warranty, septic and water details, utility costs if known.
- **Standard:** keep builder's risk or vacant-dwelling coverage in force until closing; an empty finished house needs heat, checks, and security.

### 20.12 Negotiate the offer and inspection response [T2]
- **What:** a signed purchase agreement, and an agreed response to the buyer's inspection.
- **How:** expect a buyer's inspector even on new construction. Fix real defects promptly; offer documentation, not argument, for items that are built correctly.
- **Standard:** hold a repair reserve of about 0.5–1% of price [E].

### 20.13 Close the sale [T2]
- **What:** deed recorded, construction loan paid off, proceeds received.
- **Standard:** state excise tax is graduated: 1.1% of the price up to $525,000 and 1.28% of the part from $525,000 to $1,525,000 for sales through 2026; from 1 January 2027 those thresholds are $551,000 and $1,551,000 [V]. Kitsap County adds a local 0.5% [V]. Example: a $700,000 sale in 2027 owes $6,061 plus $1,907 state and $3,500 local, $11,468 in total, about 1.64% of the price. The seller usually pays, and if the seller does not the buyer is liable [V]. The tax is paid to the county treasurer with the excise tax affidavit [V]; escrow normally does this from your proceeds at closing [E]. The sale of the real estate itself is not subject to state business tax or retail sales tax for a qualifying speculative builder [V]. Expect escrow to report the gross proceeds to the IRS on Form 1099-S [E].
- **How:** review the settlement statement line by line before signing; collect final lien waivers from every trade first.
- **Point of no return:** closing.

### 20.14 Set the warranty service plan [T2]
- **What:** a way for the buyer to report problems, and a schedule for handling them.
- **How:** give the buyer one contact and a simple request form; plan a walk-through at about 60 days and again near eleven months [E].
- **Standard:** respond in writing to every request. A buyer must serve a written notice of claim at least 45 days before filing a construction defect suit. You then have 21 days, or longer if you demand a meeting, to serve a written response that proposes an inspection, offers a settlement, or disputes the claim [V]. Send any such notice to your attorney and insurer the day it arrives. Construction claims that have not accrued within six years after substantial completion are barred; a claim that accrues inside that window still has its own limitation period [V]. That six-year rule is written for the benefit of those required to be registered or licensed, so ask the attorney whether it protects you [V]. Keep completed-operations insurance in force for the period your attorney and broker advise, and keep the project binder at least as long.

### 20.15 Produce the final profit-and-loss [T2]
- **What:** one page: sale price, less every cost by code (each including the sales tax paid on it), less commissions, excise tax, and closing costs, equals profit before income tax. Then subtract the CPA's figure for federal income tax and self-employment tax to get profit after tax, and divide that by your hours. Do not enter any value for your own labor as a cost [V].
- **Who:** send it to the CPA with the bookkeeping file within two weeks of closing, and ask for the estimated tax payment amount and due date. The payment for the quarter of the sale is the one most often missed [E]. Ask which trades need a Form 1099-NEC and file them by the January deadline [E].
- **Output:** final profit-and-loss, used in Module 21.

## Top mistakes
1. T1: selling inside the periods you committed to (20.5).
2. T1: no maintenance on the septic and the envelope (20.3).
3. T2: pricing to cost instead of the market (20.7).
4. T2: improvising disclosures and warranty (20.9).
5. Both: closing the books before the lien period runs (20.6, 20.13).

## Review notes
- Specialist review round, 15 Nov 2026: 10 corrections applied (4 from the attorney, 6 from the cpa). The reviewers were AI agents standing in for each profession and checking current sources. This is not a professional review.
- The first draft left disclosure and warranty as questions. That round replaced them with sourced rules: new construction is not exempt from seller disclosure, an implied warranty of habitability can apply, and a defect claim follows a statutory notice and cure process.
- The pre-sale tax rule was narrowed: a purchase agreement that closes after completion does not by itself change the tax treatment.
- Lender: added maturity and rate-lock timing to conversion.

## Confirm with a professional
