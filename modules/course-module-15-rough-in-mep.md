# Module 15 — Rough-In: Mechanical, Electrical, Plumbing

**Draft v1 — 15 Nov 2026 — not yet reviewed by an electrician, plumber, heating contractor, or inspector. Do not build from this draft.** [V] verified against a cited checklist, [E] trade practice. T1 owner-occupied, T2 spec.

**Outcome:** when you finish this module every pipe, duct, and wire inside the walls, floor, and attic will be installed, tested, photographed, and approved, and the framing inspection will be passed.

**Before you start:** roof dry; windows in; trade permits in hand (5.11); fixture, appliance, and equipment selections final with their installation sheets; backing installed (13.21).

**Who does the work**

- **T1:** you may do your own plumbing and electrical under owner exemptions, personally, with the occupancy commitment in Module 2 [V]. Refrigerant work on heat pumps requires federal certification; plan to hire start-up at least [E].
- **T2:** licensed electrical and plumbing contractors do this work [V]. Your role is layout, coordination, and quality checks.

**Limits of this module:** the electrical and plumbing codes are long. This module gives the sequence, the coordination, and what local checklists look for. Self-performing owners need the code book or a paid consult for sizing and layout. The state electrical rules adopt the 2023 National Electrical Code now and the 2026 edition from 31 December 2026 [V]; ask the state electrical inspector which edition your permit falls under. The plumbing code is the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code with state amendments [V].

**Order that works:** plumbing drains first (least flexible), then ducts, then water lines, then wiring (most flexible), then low-voltage.

---

### 15.1 Hold a layout walkthrough with each trade
- **What:** agreed routes and locations before anyone drills.
- **How:** walk the house with plans: main drain route and slopes; vent routes to the roof; duct trunks and returns; panel and meter location; equipment locations and clearances; every exterior penetration.
- **Standard:** resolve conflicts on paper. The usual ones: a duct and a drain wanting the same joist bay; a vent stack landing on a truss; a panel with no working space.
- **Later trades:** roof penetrations go to the roofer (13.20); exterior blocks to 14.5.

### 15.2 Mark every fixture, device, and unit
- **What:** locations marked on floors and studs with paint or marker.
- **How:** toilets, tubs, showers, sinks, laundry box, hose bibs, water heater, air handler, registers, returns, bath fans, range hood, dryer vent, panel, every outlet, switch, light, smoke and carbon monoxide alarm, thermostat, data and television points.
- **Standard:** toilet centerline at least 15 in. from each side wall with 30 in. total clear width, and 24 in. clear in front under the state plumbing code amendment now in force [V]. The county's 2018-code checklist prints 21 in.; lay out to 24. Hot control on the left [V].
- **Done when:** you can stand in each room and "use" it by pointing.

### 15.3 Set tubs, showers, and blocking
- **What:** tubs and shower pans set and protected; valve blocking and drain access in place.
- **Standard:** shower at least 900 sq in. with a 30 in. circle; rough pan at least 30 in. x 30 in.; shower door opening at least 22 in.; access panel at least 12 in. x 12 in. for a tub drain slip joint [V].
- **How:** set tubs on a mortar bed or as the maker requires; protect them with cardboard and plywood until the house is finished.

### 15.4 Rough-in drains and vents
- **What:** drain, waste, and vent piping installed, sloped, supported, and stubbed.
- **Standard [V unless noted]:**
    - Trap arm lengths limited by pipe size: 1-1/4 in. to 2 ft 6 in.; 1-1/2 in. to 3 ft 6 in.; 2 in. to 5 ft; 3 in. to 6 ft.
    - Toilet no more than 6 ft from its vent.
    - Vents rise at least 6 in. above the fixture's flood rim before running horizontal.
    - Cleanouts at the upper end of horizontal drains and at direction changes over 135 degrees; an underfloor cleanout no more than 20 ft from the crawl access, reachable with at least 18 in. of vertical and 30 in. of horizontal clearance, or else extended above the floor or outside the building.
    - Plastic pipe supported every 4 ft horizontally.
    - Nail plates of 18 gauge where pipe is within 1 in. of the framing face.
    - Laundry standpipe 18–30 in. above its trap, trap 6–18 in. above the floor.
    - Drain slope commonly 1/4 in. per foot [E].
- **Inspector checks:** sizing against the code table, slope, support, venting, cleanouts, protection plates.
- **Safety:** never notch or bore framing beyond the limits (15.12).

### 15.5 Rough-in water supply
- **What:** hot and cold lines to every fixture, with shutoffs and stub-outs secured.
- **Standard:** building shutoff is a full-way valve; pressure above 80 psi needs a regulator; hot and cold lines outside conditioned space insulated to at least R-3; water hammer arrestors where required; tub and shower valves limited to 120°F [V].
- **Options (pipe) [E]:** cross-linked polyethylene tubing (fast, forgiving, most owner-friendly); copper (durable, needs soldering skill).
- **Later trades:** secure every stub-out rigidly; finish plumbers and tile setters rely on exact positions.

### 15.6 Test the plumbing
- **What:** drains and water lines holding their tests when the inspector arrives.
- **Standard:** drain test with a 10-ft head of water for 15 minutes (air tests are not used on plastic drain pipe); water lines at working pressure or 50 psi for 15 minutes [V].
- **How:** plug the stubs, fill the system, mark the water level, and watch it yourself for a day before calling.
- **If it goes wrong:** the level drops. Find the leak at a joint or test plug; re-test.

### 15.7 Install heating and cooling rough-in
- **What:** air handler or indoor units set, ducts or refrigerant line sets and condensate drains run, thermostat wire pulled.
- **Standard:** equipment working space at least 30 in. x 30 in. on the control side; a light switch and outlet at equipment in a crawl space or attic; flexible duct supported at least every 4 ft without kinks; crawl space ducts at least 4 in. above ground; ducts in unconditioned space insulated to R-8 and sealed; register boots sealed [V].
- **Options [E]:** ducted heat pump (duct design matters; get a load and duct calculation); ductless units (line sets and condensate only; simplest for owners).
- **Later trades:** every ducted heating or cooling system must pass a leakage test wherever the ducts run (19.2): total leakage of 4 cfm per 100 sq ft of conditioned floor area, or 8 only where all ducts and the air handler are inside the thermal envelope, which ducts in a crawl space are not [V]. The test may be run at rough-in, while the joints can still be reached [V]. Seal every joint with mastic or listed tape as you go.

### 15.8 Install ventilation and exhaust
- **What:** whole-house ventilation, bath fans, range hood duct, and dryer duct, all terminated outdoors.
- **Standard:** whole-house ventilation runs continuously at the code rate unless it has approved intermittent controls; dryer duct is 4 in. smooth metal, no more than 35 ft long less 2.5 ft per 45-degree elbow and 5 ft per 90-degree elbow, unless the dryer maker allows more; exhaust ducts in unconditioned space insulated; at least 30 in. from a cooktop to unprotected combustibles above [V].
- **How:** shortest, straightest runs; slope ducts to the outside; use the blocks installed in 14.5.

### 15.9 Set the service and panel
- **What:** meter base, service entrance, main panel, and grounding connected to the electrode from 12.10.
- **Standard:** utility requirements for the meter base; working clearance in front of the panel; grounding and bonding per the electrical code [E].
- **Who:** call the state electrical inspector's office with questions before you build it; they answer owner questions.
- **Track:** T2, licensed electrician.

### 15.10 Rough-in wiring
- **What:** boxes set, cable pulled, stapled, and made up in the boxes; no devices yet.
- **Standard:** smoke alarms on every floor, in each bedroom, and in hallways serving bedrooms, interconnected and hardwired; carbon monoxide alarms on every floor and near sleeping areas; lighting and switches at stairs [V]. Box height, cable support, circuit sizing, and protection types per the electrical code [E].
- **How:** set all boxes to a consistent height with a story stick; drill holes in the center of studs; keep cable clear of where trim and cabinet screws will go.
- **Later trades:** recessed lights in insulated ceilings must be rated for insulation contact and airtight (16.2) [V].

### 15.11 Rough-in low-voltage
- **What:** data, television, doorbell, thermostat, and any security or audio cable.
- **Track:** T2 requires a licensed telecommunications or electrical contractor for new buildings intended for sale [V].
- **Later trades:** run conduit from the attic or crawl space to a central point for future cable.

### 15.12 Fire-block and seal penetrations
- **What:** every hole through a top plate, bottom plate, or fire block sealed; framing checked for damage.
- **Standard:** penetrations at plates, fire blocks, soffits, and ceiling lines sealed; rough-in work has not damaged framing; plumbing openings to the crawl space screened or collared with gaps no larger than 1/2 in. [V]. Top plates notched or bored beyond limits need a 16-gauge strap with eight 16d nails each side [V].
- **How:** walk every wall with fire-rated sealant or foam listed for the purpose.
- **If it goes wrong:** a joist or stud is over-notched. Call the engineer for a repair detail before the framing inspection.

### 15.13 Photograph every wall
- **What:** a photo set of every wall, ceiling, and floor bay, labeled by room, with a tape measure visible.
- **Standard:** enough to find any pipe, wire, or block after drywall.
- **Crew, time:** 1 person, 2 hours. This is the most valuable two hours in the binder.

### 15.14 Pass plumbing and mechanical rough inspections (wait)
- **What:** approvals with systems on test.
- **How:** request per the permit; tests holding when the inspector arrives; plans and fixture counts on site.

### 15.15 Pass electrical cover inspection (wait)
- **What:** state electrical approval before any wiring is covered.
- **Standard:** the state inspects new electrical installations; wiring must be inspected before it is concealed [V]. Kitsap County will not approve the framing inspection unless proof of the state electrical rough-in approval is on site [V]. Request the state inspection no later than three business days after finishing the work [V].
- **How:** request through the state's system; grounds made up in boxes; panel accessible.

### 15.16 Pass framing inspection (wait)
- **What:** building department approval to insulate.
- **Standard:** the county asks for this inspection after the roof, framing, fire blocking, windows, exterior siding, and bracing are in place and energy code caulking and sealing are done; plumbing and mechanical rough-in approved or requested at the same visit; proof of the state electrical rough-in approval on site [V]; truss drawings and approved plans on site; no significant moisture in the framing; stair geometry, egress windows, safety glazing, and attic and crawl access checked [V].
- **If it goes wrong:** insulation goes in before this approval. It comes back out.

## Module gate
- [ ] Plumbing, mechanical, electrical cover, and framing inspections approved
- [ ] Every penetration sealed
- [ ] Photo record complete and backed up
- [ ] No framing member damaged beyond limits
- [ ] Tubs and windows protected for the finish phase

## Top mistakes
1. Running wire first, then finding no path for a drain or duct (order of work).
2. Too little slope or venting on drains (15.4).
3. Leaky, unsealed ducts found only at the final test (15.7).
4. Insulating before the framing inspection (15.16).
5. No photos before cover (15.13).
