# Module 19 — Final Inspections and Occupancy

**Draft v1 — 15 Nov 2026 — not yet reviewed by an inspector.** [V] verified against the county's final inspection checklist (written to the 2018 codes), [E] estimate. T1 owner-occupied, T2 spec.

**Outcome:** when you finish this module every permit will be closed, the building final approved, and you will hold the certificate of occupancy and a complete closeout binder.

**Before you start:** pre-punch list complete (17.14); site work complete (Module 18); permit tracker (5.1) showing every open permit and condition.

---

### 19.1 Build the final checklist from every permit
- **What:** one list of everything that must be approved, tested, paid, or posted before final.
- **How:** combine the permit conditions, the tracker, and the county's final inspection checklist. Mark each item open or closed.
- **Standard:** all prior required inspections approved and prior correction notices addressed [V].

### 19.2 Complete third-party tests (wait)
- **What:** a signed blower door report, a duct leakage report if the house has any heating or cooling ducts, and measured ventilation fan flows.
- **Standard:** blower door report signed by the testing agency and given to the code official. Under the 2021 state energy code now in force the limit is 4.0 air changes per hour at 50 pascals on every compliance path, and lower if you claimed an air-leakage credit [V]. The county's final checklist still prints the 2018-code figure of 5; do not rely on it. Duct leakage report signed by the tester for every ducted heating or cooling system: total leakage no more than 4 cfm per 100 sq ft of conditioned floor area at 25 pascals, or 8 where all ducts and the air handler are inside the thermal envelope; ducts in a crawl space never qualify for the 8 [V]. Whole-house and local exhaust fans must be flow-tested to their required rates [V]. Your approved energy form states the numbers you must hit.
- **How:** book the tester two weeks out; before they arrive, close windows, fill plumbing traps, and install the attic hatch and weather-stripping.
- **If it goes wrong:** the house fails. With the fan running, find leaks with smoke or by hand: attic hatch, recessed lights, plumbing and electrical penetrations, the rim, door thresholds. Seal and re-test.

### 19.3 Post the energy certificate
- **What:** the completed certificate posted near the furnace or in the utility room.
- **Standard:** required at final [V]. It lists insulation values, window ratings, equipment efficiencies, and test results; gather them from 16.12 and 17.11.

### 19.4 Pass the electrical final (wait)
- **What:** state electrical permit approved and closed.
- **Standard:** the building final requires the state electrical permit approved and finaled, with the state's sticker on the panel [V].
- **How:** all devices and fixtures installed, panel labeled, power on. Request through the state's system.

### 19.5 Close septic, water, and road approvals (wait)
- **What:** sign-offs from the health district and any other agency. The health district final for septic and well must be signed off before you request the county final, and any stormwater documentation your permit conditions require must be uploaded to the portal and reviewed first [V].
- **Standard:** separate sewer or septic permit signed off by its authority; backflow devices installed and tested where present, with third-party test reports [V].

### 19.6 Pay deferred fees
- **What:** any deferred impact fees and outstanding permit fees paid.
- **Standard:** deferred impact fees must be paid before the final inspection is scheduled [V]. The county's 2026 example shows $7,619 in impact fees for a 2,000 sq ft house [V].

### 19.7 Self-inspect against the county final checklist
- **What:** your own pass through the full checklist, with corrections made.
- **Items owner-builders most often miss [V]:**
    - House numbers at least 4 in. tall, contrasting.
    - Grade falling 6 in. in the first 10 ft; siding 6 in. above soil.
    - Attic: access at least 22 in. x 30 in., insulated and gasketed, with a curb; depth markers visible; baffles clear.
    - Crawl space: access at least 18 in. x 24 in.; vents unobstructed; ground cover wall to wall; debris removed; underfloor cleanout reachable.
    - Stairs, handrails, and guards to the figures in 17.7.
    - Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms in every required location, interconnected.
    - Bedroom egress windows operable without tools; safety glazing marked.
    - Garage: self-closing, rated or solid door to the house; gypsum board separation; no duct openings into the garage.
    - Water heater: relief valve and drain, two seismic straps, pan and expansion tank where required.
    - Plumbing vents at least 6 in. above the roof; hose bibs with vacuum breakers.
    - Dryer duct within length limits; range clearances met.
    - Ducts in unconditioned space insulated to R-8 and sealed; whole-house ventilation running.
    - Decks: ledger, lateral connection, guards, and footings as built per plans.
    - A heat source in each habitable room; the primary heat source is not a wood stove.
- **Done when:** every line is checked and photographed.

### 19.8 Pass the building final (wait)
- **What:** approved final inspection.
- **How:** request once 19.2–19.7 are complete. Have on site: permit and approved plans, test reports, special inspection final letter if applicable, energy certificate posted, a ladder for attic access.
- **Standard:** the inspector runs every fixture, checks every item above, and looks at the attic and crawl space [V].
- **If it goes wrong:** a correction notice. Fix, photograph, and request a re-inspection; ask whether a fee applies.

### 19.9 Obtain the certificate of occupancy
- **What:** the county's document allowing the house to be occupied.
- **How:** in Kitsap County the last county inspection is the Final/Occupancy inspection, and its approval is the approval to occupy the home; occupying before it is approved is a civil infraction [V]. The county fee schedule lists a certificate of occupancy ($145) and a temporary certificate of occupancy ($290) [V]. Ask the permit center whether a printed certificate for a house is produced automatically or on request, because lenders ask for the document [E].
- **Standard:** do not move in, list for sale as complete, or close a sale before it is issued [E].
- **Track:** T1 lenders need it to convert the loan; T2 buyers' lenders need it to close.

### 19.10 Assemble the closeout binder
- **What:** the finished project record.
- **Contents:** permits and final approvals; certificate of occupancy; approved plans and as-builts (foundation, utilities); septic design and as-built; well log or water documents; test reports; energy certificate copy; wall photos; product manuals and warranties; trade contact list; lien waivers; final cost report.
- **Track:** T2 prepares a copy for the buyer.

## Top mistakes
1. Requesting final with another permit still open (19.4, 19.5).
2. Failing the blower door test with finishes complete (19.2).
3. Unpaid deferred fees blocking the inspection (19.6).
4. Small life-safety items: alarms, rails, numbers, water heater straps (19.7).
