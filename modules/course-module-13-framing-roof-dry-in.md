# Module 13 — Framing, Roof, and Dry-In

**Draft v1 — 15 Nov 2026 — not yet reviewed by a framer, engineer, or inspector. Do not build from this draft.** [V] verified against a cited checklist, [E] trade practice. Where this module and the stamped plans disagree, the plans govern. T1 owner-occupied, T2 spec.

**Assumed build:** single story, crawl space, dimensional or I-joist floor on a mudsill, 2x6 exterior walls, engineered trusses, composition shingles.

**Outcome:** when you finish this module you will have a framed, braced, inspected structure with a finished roof: weather-tight from above and ready for windows and rough-in.

**Before you start:** foundation as-built (12.19), structural sheets, shear wall and hold-down schedule, truss drawings, lumber and hardware on order (9.6), fall protection (8.6).

**Inspections:** underfloor (13.6); sheathing and shear (13.18). The framing inspection comes after rough-in (15.16).

**Points of no return:** subfloor over an uninspected floor system (13.7); roof sheathing over mis-set trusses (13.17).

**Never change in the field without the engineer:** trusses, engineered beams and I-joists, hold-downs and their anchors, shear wall nailing and sheathing, post and beam sizes, anchor bolts, and any opening added or enlarged in a shear wall.

**Variant — prescriptive wall bracing:** this module assumes engineered shear walls. If your plans use the code's braced wall panels instead, 13.9 and 13.12 change: panel locations and lengths come from the bracing plan, fastening from the code table, and anchorage from the bracing method. That variant is not written in this draft; have the designer mark every braced panel on the plans.

**Track note:** on T2, self-performed framing on a house built for sale falls under the contractor rules in Module 2.

---

### 13.1 Review framing plans and build lists
- **What:** a cut list, a hardware list by location, and a marked-up plan showing every shear wall, hold-down, beam, and point load.
- **How:** highlight shear walls by type on the floor plan; write the nailing for each beside it; trace every point load from roof to foundation.
- **Standard:** point loads continue to the foundation [V].
- **Done when:** you can state, for any wall, its type, nailing, and hardware without opening the engineering.

### 13.2 Order lumber and confirm trusses
- **What:** the lumber package scheduled in drops (floor, walls, roof), and a confirmed truss delivery date and truck access.
- **Standard:** sheathing grade and thickness match the plans [V]. Order pressure-treated plates and the correct fasteners for them.
- **If it goes wrong:** everything arrives at once and sits in the rain. Stage deliveries.

### 13.3 Install the mudsill
- **What:** pressure-treated sill plates bolted to the foundation, straight and square, over a sill gasket.
- **How:** 1) Snap lines square to the as-built, correcting small foundation errors. 2) Lay out and drill for anchor bolts with a bit no more than 1/16 in. larger than the bolt; an oversized or slotted hole lets the wall slide before the bolt takes load [E]. 3) Place sill seal. 4) Set plates, washers, and nuts. 5) Shim low spots with full-width, non-compressible shims at every bolt and under every post and stud pack, and grout gaps over about 1/4 in.; check level [E]. 6) Snug the nuts, then check them again just before cover because the plate shrinks as it dries [E]. Never notch the plate, slot a hole, bend a bolt, or leave one out to make the plate fit.
- **Standard:** plates on concrete are treated or naturally decay-resistant wood [V]. At least two bolts per plate piece, no more than 6 ft apart, within 12 in. of each end and at least 7 bolt diameters from the end, in the middle third of the plate [V]. Plate washers at least 3 in. x 3 in. x 0.229 in., hot-dipped galvanized on treated plates; slotted washers need a standard cut washer under the nut [V]. Level within 1/8 in. over 10 ft (craft) [E].
- **Inspector checks:** bolt size and spacing against the shear schedule; washers; nuts tightened [V].
- **Crew, time:** 2 people, 1 day.
- **If it goes wrong:** a bolt lands at a plate end or is missing. Do not improvise; an added anchor in cured concrete may need the engineer and a special inspection (12.15).

### 13.4 Set posts, beams, and girders
- **What:** interior supports installed on pier pads with positive connections.
- **Standard:** posts connected positively to pads and beams; girder joints over supports; beams at least 12 in. and joists at least 18 in. above grade unless treated [V]. Posts on concrete need a barrier or pedestal per plan [V].
- **How:** set beams to a string line at mudsill height; crown up. Build every beam, post, and connector exactly as sized on the plans: a different species or grade, a smaller or different engineered beam, or a different connector needs the engineer's written approval first [E]. Glulam, LVL, and PSL beams are not notched or drilled except as the maker's guide allows, and a glulam goes in with its stamped top up [E]. Where a beam sits in a concrete pocket, bear on at least 3 in. and leave 1/2 in. of air space at the sides and end, or use treated wood [E]. Under every point load marked on the plans, carry a full-width post or squash blocks through the floor framing to the pier or footing sized for it [E].

### 13.5 Frame the floor
- **What:** joists, rim, blocking, and hangers installed per the layout.
- **How:** mark layout on the plates; crown joists up; install hangers with the specified nails; block at bearing points.
- **Standard:** joist bearing at least 1-1/2 in. on wood or steel and 3 in. on concrete; joists lapped at least 3 in. over supports; solid blocking at ends and bearing points; I-joists installed to the manufacturer's guide, which must be on site [V]. Never cut or notch an I-joist flange; holes go only in the web and only where the maker's hole chart allows [E]. Sawn joists: notches no deeper than one-sixth of the joist depth and none in the middle third of the span, end notches no deeper than one-quarter of the depth, holes no larger than one-third of the depth and at least 2 in. from the top and bottom edges [V]. Trusses, glulam, LVL, and I-joists are not cut, notched, or drilled at all unless the design or the maker allows that exact hole [V].
- **Later trades:** check toilet, tub, and floor register locations against the joist layout now; a flange centered on a joist is a classic conflict [V]. Leave plumbing chases clear.
- **Crew, time:** 2–3 people, 3–5 days [E].

### 13.6 Pass underfloor inspection (wait)
- **What:** approval of the floor frame, and of any plumbing, ducts, or wiring under the floor, before the subfloor covers it.
- **How:** complete under-floor plumbing and duct runs that will be hard to reach later; have them inspected with or before the framing check.
- **Inspector checks:** clearances to grade; hardware and anchor bolts; hangers; blocking; foundation undamaged by backfill; crawl vents at 1 sq ft per 300 sq ft of underfloor area [V].
- **If it goes wrong:** you sheath first. The inspector can require openings cut in the new subfloor.

### 13.7 Sheath the subfloor
- **What:** subfloor panels glued and fastened.
- **How:** run panels perpendicular to joists; stagger end joints; apply subfloor adhesive to each joist just ahead of the panel; fasten per the schedule; leave the manufacturer's gap between panels.
- **Standard:** fastener type and spacing per plans; a 1/8 in. gap at panel edges unless the panel is self-spacing [E].
- **Weather:** a subfloor exposed for weeks in rain swells at the edges. Use panels rated for exposure, sweep standing water, and drill drain holes if water ponds [E].

### 13.8 Lay out walls
- **What:** every wall, opening, and stud location marked on the subfloor and plates.
- **How:** 1) Snap lines for all walls. 2) Check diagonals of the whole floor. 3) Cut top and bottom plates in pairs and mark layout on both together. 4) Mark windows, doors, posts, hold-down studs, and intersecting walls.
- **Standard:** lines square within 1/8 in.; layout pulled from the same end on every wall so sheathing joints land on studs [E].
- **Later trades:** mark tub and shower valve locations and cabinet runs so studs do not land in the way.

### 13.9 Frame and raise exterior walls
- **What:** exterior walls built flat, sheathed where practical, and stood up.
- **How:** build headers and trimmers first; nail the wall together on the deck; square it by diagonals; sheath and nail per the shear schedule while it is flat; raise with enough people or wall jacks; brace immediately. Before sheathing flat, read the shear wall details: where they show sheathing lapping down over the rim joist or sill, or a blocked horizontal joint at a set height, let the panels hang below the bottom plate by that amount or sheath that wall after it is standing. Panels cut flush with the bottom plate cannot be fixed later [E]. Leave a 1/8 in. gap between panels [V]. Build in lengths the crew can lift: a sheathed 2x6 wall weighs roughly 30 to 40 lb per foot of length, so four people should stop at about 20 ft or use wall jacks [E].
- **Standard:** double or triple trimmers under headers per the tables and plans; stud size, grade, and spacing per plans [V].
- **Safety:** a 2x6 sheathed wall is heavy. Plan each lift, clear the fall zone, and nail stop blocks at the deck edge so the plate cannot kick out.
- **Crew, time:** 3–4 people on lift days.

### 13.10 Frame interior walls
- **What:** interior partitions and bearing walls stood and tied in.
- **Standard:** bearing walls and posts line up with the supports below.

### 13.11 Plumb, line, and brace
- **What:** walls plumb at corners, straight along the top, and held by temporary braces.
- **How:** plumb corners with a long level or laser; string the top plates and push or pull to the line; install the double top plate with laps at corners and intersections.
- **Standard:** plumb within 1/4 in. in 8 ft; top plate straight within 1/4 in. along its length (craft) [E]. End joints in the two top plates are offset at least 24 in. as a code floor. Engineered plans in this seismic category usually call for a 48 in. lap with a set number of nails, because the top plates carry earthquake force along the wall line; follow the plan detail [E]. The 16-gauge by 1-1/2 in. metal tie is a different rule: it is required where a top plate is cut or bored more than half its width for pipe or duct, and it does not replace the splice offset [V].

### 13.12 Install hold-downs, straps, and shear nailing
- **What:** all lateral hardware and shear nailing complete.
- **How:** work the shear schedule wall by wall and check off each.
- **Standard:** common nails of the size on the shear schedule, not box or sinker nails; panel edges blocked or landing on framing; full-height studs at hold-downs and straps, with multiple studs where specified; nails in every hold-down stud; hardware installed to the plans and maker's instructions [V]. Drive nails flush, at least 3/8 in. from the panel edge [E]. Up to 20% of the nails around a panel's perimeter may be overdriven by no more than 1/8 in.; past that, add one nail for every two overdriven, or get the engineer's fix [V]. Where the schedule calls for 3x studs or plates, or doubled studs, at closely nailed panel joints, they are required and the nails are staggered [E]. Sheathing edge nailing goes into the hold-down post itself, and each hold-down gets the exact screws, bolts, or nails the maker lists, in every hole [E]. No substitutions: a different panel thickness or grade, nail, stud size, hold-down, or strap changes the wall's capacity and needs the engineer's written approval. Do not notch or bore a hold-down post [E].
- **Inspector checks:** this list, on the sheathing and shear inspection, before anything covers it [V].
- **If it goes wrong:** the nail gun is set too deep and breaks the panel face. Stop, adjust, and ask the engineer how to correct the overdriven area.

### 13.13 Set trusses
- **What:** trusses set at layout, plumb, and temporarily braced.
- **How:** 1) Mark layout on the top plates. 2) Stage trusses flat and supported. 3) Lift by crane or boom truck. 4) Set and brace the first truss rigidly to the ground or deck. 5) Set each next truss with spacers and lateral braces as you go.
- **Standard:** truss drawings on site, stamped; configuration, bearing, and hangers per the drawings; truss members are never cut, notched, drilled, spliced, or altered without written approval from a registered design professional, and loads the truss was not designed for (air handler, water heater, storage platform, solar) are not added without the same check [V]. A truss that arrives or becomes cracked, has a loose or missing plate, or is broken in handling is set aside; get a sealed repair detail from the truss supplier before it is installed or covered [E].
- **Safety:** unbraced trusses fall like dominoes. Follow the truss industry's bracing guide (BCSI) that ships with the package. Its core rules: ground bracing is built before the first truss is lifted and the first truss is fastened to it; rows of top chord lateral restraint are no more than 10 ft apart for spans up to 30 ft and 8 ft apart for spans of 30 to 45 ft; restraints are 2x4 or larger and lap over at least two trusses; diagonal braces go on the top and bottom chord planes at least every 10 truss spaces (20 ft); bottom chord restraint rows are 10 to 15 ft apart [V]. Spacer blocks alone are not bracing: without diagonals the whole set racks over together [E]. Do not stack plywood or OSB more than 16 in. high on trusses, and never at midspan or the peak [V]. Do not set trusses in gusty wind, and do not release the crane until the truss is restrained [E]. No one stands under a lifted truss.
- **Crew, time, tools:** 3–4 people plus crane operator; 1 day for a simple roof.
- **Money out:** crane, by the hour.

### 13.14 Install permanent truss bracing
- **What:** all permanent bracing, blocking, and tie-downs shown on the truss drawings and plans.
- **Standard:** bracing complete per the truss engineer's plans; ganged trusses fastened per the manufacturer; roof-to-wall connectors per plans [V].
- **Load path:** this is where roof forces reach the walls: blocking between truss heels with the nailing or clips on the plans, an uplift connector at every truss, gable end bracing, and any drag straps. It is the item most often missing at the sheathing inspection [E].
- **Later trades:** frame the attic access now: at least 22 in. x 30 in. with 30 in. of headroom above [V].

### 13.15 Set up scaffold and staging
- **What:** a safe working platform at the eaves and gables for roof edge framing, sheathing, roofing, and later the siding.
- **How:** 1) Choose the system. 2) Set it on firm, level bearing. 3) Tie it to the building as the maker requires. 4) Fit guardrails and access. 5) Inspect it before each shift and after wind.
- **Options:** pump jacks with aluminum poles (cheap to own, good for long straight walls, two people to set); rented frame scaffold (stable, slow to move); towable boom lift (fast on rough ground, one or two workers only, weekly rent); wall brackets and planks (framers' method; needs experience).
- **Recommendation:** rented frame scaffold or pump jacks on the eave walls, plus a boom lift for the week of gable and rake work [E].
- **Standard:** Washington's fall protection rules apply from 4 ft on most surfaces (8.6). Guardrails, a personal fall arrest system, or both, by the system and task.
- **Money out:** rent it through siding (Module 14), not just through roofing. Returning and re-renting costs more than holding it [E].
- **Done when:** every roof edge can be reached from a guarded platform or a tied-off position.

### 13.16 Frame eaves and rakes
- **What:** truss tails trimmed, sub-fascia and fascia on, rake (gable overhang) framing built, and eave blocking installed.
- **How:** 1) Snap a line across the truss tails and cut them to it. 2) Install the sub-fascia, straightened to a string. 3) Build the rake overhangs as the truss drawings and plans show (outlookers or a dropped gable truss). 4) Install the eave blocking between trusses shown on the plans, using vented blocks where the attic vents through them. 5) Install and prime the fascia and rake boards.
- **Standard:** blocking and its nailing per the plans. The roof edge nailing into that blocking is part of the lateral design and is slow to add from below later [E].
- **Why now:** drip edge laps over the fascia (13.19), and roof sheathing runs out over the rake framing (13.17). Neither can wait for exterior trim (14.9).
- **Crew, time:** 2 people, 1–2 days on a simple gable roof [E].
- **Done when:** fascia is straight to a string, rakes are built, and every blocking bay on the plans is filled.

### 13.17 Sheath the roof
- **What:** roof panels installed and nailed.
- **How:** eave and rake framing complete first (13.16). Start square to the eave; stagger joints; leave a 1/8 in. gap at panel ends and edges unless the panel maker says otherwise [V]; use panel clips where specified; nail per the schedule.
- **Standard:** fasteners per plan. Leave the ridge or other vent openings the roof design calls for.
- **Safety:** fall protection from the first sheet.

### 13.18 Pass sheathing and shear inspection (wait)
- **What:** approval of sheathing, shear nailing, and hardware.
- **Standard:** this inspection occurs before any water-resistive barrier, cladding, window or door flashing, or insulation goes on [V].
- **How:** leave all wall sheathing and hardware exposed; have plans and truss drawings on site. When you request it, ask whether the inspector also wants to see roof sheathing nailing before underlayment covers it: the county checklist covers diaphragm nailing but does not mention roofing underlayment [V]. If so, request the inspection for the day roof sheathing finishes and ask whether underlayment may go on first, with photos, when rain is forecast [E].
- **If it goes wrong:** wrap goes on first to beat the rain. You will be removing it.

### 13.19 Dry in the roof
- **What:** drip edge and underlayment installed over the whole roof.
- **How:** fascia and rake (barge) boards must already be on, straight, and primed, because drip edge laps over them (13.16); they cannot wait for 14.9. Then eave drip edge, then underlayment lapped shingle-fashion from the eave up, then rake drip edge over the underlayment [E]. Use synthetic underlayment rated for the weeks of exposure you expect, fastened with cap fasteners; felt wrinkles and tears when it sits through rain [E].
- **Standard:** follow the roofing manufacturer's instructions; they are what the warranty and the inspector rely on.
- **Weather:** this is the dry-in milestone. Schedule it the same day or the day after sheathing.

### 13.20 Install roofing and flashings
- **What:** shingles, valley and wall flashings, pipe boots, and vents.
- **Options:** DIY (large savings; real fall risk); hire a roofer (fast, warranted, often one to two days).
- **Recommendation:** hire it unless the roof is low-slope and simple and you have fall arrest gear and experience.
- **Standard:** step flashing at every wall, kick-out flashing where a roof edge meets a wall, and plumbing vents extending at least 6 in. above the roof [V for vent height; E otherwise].
- **Later trades:** all roof penetrations (plumbing vents, exhaust vents) should be located before roofing. Coordinate with 15.1.

### 13.21 Install blocking and backing for later trades
- **What:** every piece of backing the finishes will need.
- **List [E]:** cabinets (upper and lower rails); bath grab bars and towel bars; shower doors; tub and shower edges; handrails; wall-hung televisions and shelves; closet rods; curtain rods; stair and guard posts; drywall backing at corners and ceilings; fire blocking at soffits and dropped ceilings; crawl access framed at least 18 in. x 24 in. [V].
- **How:** walk each room with the cabinet and fixture plans and a can of marking paint.
- **Standard:** photograph every wall with a tape in frame before cover (15.13).

### 13.22 Close out
- **What:** dry-in checklist complete, lumber returns made, schedule and cost report updated, draw requested.
- **Standard:** framing lumber should be drying. Wet framing delays insulation and drywall; the framing inspection looks for significant moisture [V]. Buy a pin moisture meter and log readings at bottom plates, sills, and north walls. A working target is 19% or less before insulation, and lower before drywall and wood flooring [E]. After a wet-season frame, budget two to four weeks of fans, dehumidifiers, and heat once the house is closed in [E].

## Module gate
- [ ] Underfloor and sheathing/shear inspections approved
- [ ] Every shear wall and hold-down checked off against the schedule
- [ ] Trusses permanently braced per the drawings
- [ ] Roof finished or fully dried in
- [ ] Backing installed and photographed

## Top mistakes
1. Covering the floor or walls before inspection (13.6, 13.18).
2. Wrong nails or overdriven nails in shear walls (13.12).
3. Unbraced trusses (13.13).
4. Missing kick-out and step flashing (13.20).
5. No backing for cabinets and rails (13.21).
