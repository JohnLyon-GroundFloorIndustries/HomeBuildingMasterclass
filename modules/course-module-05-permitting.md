# Module 5 — Permitting and Approvals

**Draft v1 — 15 Nov 2026 — not yet reviewed by a professional.** [V] verified, [E] estimate. T1 owner-occupied, T2 spec. Local content is for unincorporated Kitsap County; cities differ.

**Outcome:** when you finish this module you will hold an issued building permit and every related approval, with a tracker showing each fee, condition, and expiry date.

**Before you start:** frozen plan set (Module 4), septic design, survey, legal scope matrix (Module 2).

---

### 5.1 List every permit and approval
- **What:** a tracker with one row per approval: agency, what it needs, fee, status, expiry.
- **Typical rows:** building permit; health district septic approval; water availability or well; stormwater worksheet and erosion plan; road approach; site development activity permit if triggered; electrical permit (state); plumbing and mechanical; septic installation permit; fire marshal review; any critical area review.
- **Output:** permit tracker.

### 5.2 Talk to the building department before applying
- **What:** notes confirming the submittal list and review path for your lot.
- **How:** contact the permit center with your parcel number and site concept. Ask the script questions below.
- **Standard:** the county accepts permit applications online only [V].
- **Done when:** you have the project-specific checklist from the permit portal.

### 5.3 Obtain health district approval (wait)
- **What:** an approved Building Site Application for the septic system.
- **How:** your licensed designer submits it; a health district inspector visits the site, listed as within 30 business days; the approved application is then uploaded to the building permit [V].
- **Standard:** the building department expects this approval with the application [V].
- **If it goes wrong:** the design is rejected or needs revision. Redesign fees apply. Do not submit the building permit on an unapproved septic layout.

### 5.4 Document water supply (wait)
- **What:** a water availability letter from the purveyor, or health district approval of the well.
- **How:** the health district inspector sends a water supply checklist during septic review [V]. Complete it.

### 5.5 Complete the stormwater worksheet and erosion plan
- **What:** the Residential Stormwater Worksheet and a stormwater pollution prevention plan.
- **How:** 1) Total new hard surface: roof, driveway, parking, patios, walks, and off-site access. 2) Total land disturbed and grading volume. 3) Follow the worksheet to the required level of review.
- **Standard:** the erosion plan is required on all projects [V]. A site development activity permit is required when the worksheet classes the project as large (5,000 sq ft or more of hard surface on parcels in urbanized areas, 10,000 sq ft or more in rural areas), when grading reaches 150 cubic yards, and for work on steep slopes or near wetlands, streams, or shorelines [V]. The county fee schedule lists the single-family version of that permit at $2,900 [V]. Ask at 5.2 how the foundation excavation counts toward the 150 cubic yards [E].
- **Options:** shrink hard surface (shorter driveway, permeable surfacing where accepted) to stay under a threshold, or hire a civil engineer when you cross it.
- **If it goes wrong:** a long driveway pushes a simple house into engineered drainage. Find this out here, not at review.

### 5.6 Prepare the building site plan
- **What:** a scaled site plan meeting the county checklist.
- **How:** use the county's site plan checklist line by line: property lines, setbacks, structures, driveway, septic components, well, utilities, hard surfaces, drainage, critical areas, erosion control.
- **Options:** surveyor-prepared; self-prepared; or the septic design plan if it includes every checklist item [V].
- **Standard:** missing or wrong checklist items are a common reason submittals are returned [V].

### 5.7 Apply for the road approach permit (wait)
- **What:** approval for the driveway connection.
- **Standard:** required to build a new driveway or significantly change one [V].

### 5.8 Submit the building permit application
- **What:** a complete online application.
- **How:** upload plans, structural calculations, truss drawings, energy worksheet, site plan, stormwater worksheet, erosion plan, health approval, and water documentation. Name files as the portal asks.
- **Standard:** a certificate of concurrency is required before the county accepts a building permit application unless the project is exempt [V]. Ask at 5.2 whether your house is exempt.
- **Track:** T2 lists the registered contractor of record; the county must verify that registration before it issues the permit [V]. T1 completes the owner-builder declaration. Treat it as a statement of fact about who will do the work and why you are building: a permit obtained by falsifying information to claim a registration exemption is forfeited [V].
- **Point of no return:** the application date helps determine which code edition applies. Washington has been on the 2021 codes since 15 March 2024 [V]. The State Building Code Council's schedule, revised 14 August 2026, sets Monday 3 May 2027 as the implementation date for the 2024 codes, with the final rule filing targeted for 18 November 2026 [V]. The date has moved before. Confirm with the permit center which edition a complete application filed on your date falls under.
- **Done when:** the portal shows the application accepted as complete.

### 5.9 Respond to review comments (wait)
- **What:** resubmittals that clear every comment.
- **How:** answer each comment in a numbered letter; cloud every change on the plans; resubmit once, completely.
- **Standard:** partial responses restart the queue [E].
- **Crew, time:** review commonly takes two to six months in total [E]. Check the county's posted timeframes.

### 5.10 Pay fees and receive the permit
- **What:** issued permit and approved, stamped plans.
- **Standard:** the county's 2026 example for a 2,000 sq ft, 3-bedroom house shows $7,899 in building permit fees and $7,619 in impact fees (roads $5,059, schools $1,680, parks $880) [V]. Impact fees can be deferred but must be paid before the final inspection is scheduled [V].
- **Options:** pay impact fees now, or defer and budget them at 19.6.
- **Output:** permit, approved plans, conditions list. Read every condition.

### 5.11 Obtain trade and septic installation permits
- **What:** electrical, plumbing, mechanical, and septic installation permits.
- **How:**
    - Electrical: purchased from the state. T1 owners use the property owner application and sign the occupancy affidavit [V]. Permits are valid for one year [V].
    - Septic installation: requires the building permit number [V].
    - Plumbing and mechanical: for a new single-family house these are covered by the county building permit and its fee, not bought separately [V]. The county inspects them as Plumbing Rough-In and Mechanical Rough-In (15.14) and again at final [V].
    - Timing: buy the electrical permit before the footing pour. The state inspector, not the county, inspects the grounding electrode (12.10), temporary power (11.5), and the service trench (11.13, 11.14).
- **Track:** T2 trade permits are pulled by the licensed contractors.
- **If it goes wrong:** an electrical permit expires mid-build. Calendar the date.

### 5.12 Set up the site for inspections
- **What:** address posted, permit and approved plans on site and legible, portal account working.
- **Standard:** inspectors check for the posted address and for the permit and approved plans on site [V]. The county ended virtual inspections on 1 July 2025 [V].
- **How:** build a weatherproof plan box at the site entrance. Add every inspection to the schedule as a hold point (9.3). Request county inspections in the Online Permit Center or at 360-337-5777; the county aims to inspect within two business days and sends no separate confirmation, and final or occupancy inspections cannot be booked for the next day [V]. A building permit expires 180 days after the last approved county inspection [V].
- **Output:** inspection list from the permit, in order.

## Module gate
- [ ] Building permit issued; conditions read
- [ ] Septic design approved and installation permit path known
- [ ] Water documented
- [ ] Stormwater level of review settled
- [ ] Every permit's expiry date on the calendar
- [ ] No ground disturbed before this gate

## Top mistakes
1. Applying before septic approval (5.3).
2. Incomplete submittal or partial responses (5.8, 5.9).
3. Disturbing the site before the permit and erosion control (5.12).
4. Letting a permit expire (5.11).

## Call script — permit center
"I'm planning one single-family home on parcel [number], on septic, about [size]. Which permits will I need besides the building permit? Is the project exempt from concurrency? Which code edition applies if I apply by [date]? What is the current review time?"
