# Module 18 — Exterior Finish, Flatwork, and Landscaping

**Draft v1 — 15 Nov 2026 — not yet reviewed by a builder or inspector.** [V] verified, [E] trade practice. T1 owner-occupied, T2 spec.

**Outcome:** when you finish this module the site will drain away from the house, the driveway and walks will be in, the approved stormwater and soil measures will be complete, and the lot will be stabilized so erosion control can come out.

**Before you start:** siding and gutters complete (Module 14); utility trenches backfilled and inspected (11.14); approved site plan, stormwater worksheet, and permit conditions in hand.

**Timing:** flatwork and planting are weather-dependent. If occupancy lands in winter, ask the county what it will accept for stabilization and whether any exterior items may be completed in spring.

---

### 18.1 Final grade
- **What:** soil shaped to its finished contours around the house and across the site.
- **How:** set grade stakes from the house outward; cut swales where shown; keep soil below the siding and off the foundation vents.
- **Standard:** ground falls at least 6 in. within the first 10 ft from the foundation; where that is not possible, at least 5% slope, with swales at 2% or more [V]. Siding stays at least 6 in. above soil [V].
- **Crew, time, tools:** 1 operator and 1 person on the ground; 1–2 days; small excavator or skid steer, laser, rakes.
- **If it goes wrong:** landscaping soil is later piled against the house. Set the final grade low enough to allow for topsoil and mulch.

### 18.2 Install roof-drain and stormwater measures
- **What:** downspouts connected to the approved dispersal or infiltration measures; any other stormwater features on the approved plan built.
- **How:** follow the approved stormwater worksheet and site plan: splash blocks, dispersion trenches, drywells, rain gardens, or tight lines, as approved.
- **Standard:** what was approved is what gets built; changes need the county's agreement [E].
- **Inspector checks:** stormwater measures and permit conditions at final.

### 18.3 Pour driveway, walks, and patio
- **What:** flatwork placed on a compacted base with joints and slope.
- **How:** excavate to depth; compact base rock; form with slope away from the house; place reinforcement if specified; pour, screed, float, edge, joint, broom finish; cure.
- **Standard:** slope about 1/8 to 1/4 in. per foot for drainage; control joints at a spacing of about 2 to 3 times the slab thickness in feet (a 4-in. slab jointed every 8–12 ft, with 8–10 ft the safer end); joints cut or tooled to one quarter of the slab depth, panels close to square, tooled during finishing or sawn the same day as soon as the surface will not ravel; 4 in. thick for walks and patios, 5 to 6 in. for a driveway that will carry delivery, propane, or septic pump trucks; isolation joint material where flatwork meets the foundation, with the slab surface at least 2 in. below the siding (14.7). House plans rarely specify flatwork, so ask the supplier for an exterior, air-entrained mix of at least 3,000 psi [E].
- **Options:** hire a flatwork crew (recommended for the driveway; finishing is one-chance work at scale); pour small walks and pads yourselves; gravel driveway now and paving later where the approved plan allows.
- **Standard (stormwater):** the hard surface area you build must not exceed what the stormwater worksheet assumed [V for the worksheet basis].
- **Point of no return:** the pour.
- **Weather:** not in rain or freezing conditions; protect and cure per the supplier.

### 18.4 Complete the road approach (wait)
- **What:** the driveway connection to the road built and approved under the road approach permit.
- **Standard:** required for a new driveway [V]. Culvert, paving, and sight-distance details come from the permit.

### 18.5 Restore soil quality
- **What:** stockpiled topsoil or compost-amended soil placed over disturbed areas to the depth the county requires.
- **Standard:** the county publishes a post-construction soil quality and depth worksheet [V]. Complete it if your permit conditions require it.
- **How:** loosen compacted subsoil first; spread topsoil saved in 11.6; add compost as the worksheet specifies.

### 18.6 Plant and mulch
- **What:** required landscaping installed; all bare soil seeded, planted, or mulched.
- **Options:** seed and mulch (cheapest, season-dependent); sod (instant, costly); native plantings and bark (low maintenance).
- **Track:** T2 budgets curb appeal deliberately: a clean lawn or beds at the front, a defined entry, and fresh bark. It is a sales cost with a return.

### 18.7 Install address numbers and mailbox
- **What:** house numbers visible from the road; mailbox set per the postal carrier's requirements.
- **Standard:** numbers at least 4 in. tall in a contrasting color [V]. On a long driveway, post numbers at the road as well [E].

### 18.8 Remove erosion control
- **What:** silt fence and temporary measures removed once the site is stabilized.
- **Standard:** remove only after vegetation or cover is established and the inspector agrees [E].

### 18.9 Finish the septic approval (wait)
- **What:** final health district sign-off on the septic system and connection.
- **Standard:** the building final requires the separate sewer or septic permit to be signed off by its authority [V].
- **How:** confirm the as-built record is filed; keep risers accessible and the drainfield free of vehicles, structures, and deep-rooted plantings.

### 18.10 Close out
- **What:** photos of finished grades and stormwater features; soil worksheet filed; cost and schedule updated.

## Top mistakes
1. Finish grade too high against siding (18.1).
2. Building more hard surface than was approved (18.3).
3. Parking or landscaping on the drainfield (18.9).
4. Leaving exterior work for after move-in with no plan the county accepts.
