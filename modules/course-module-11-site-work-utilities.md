# Module 11 — Site Work and Utilities

**Draft v1 — 15 Nov 2026 — not yet reviewed by a builder, septic professional, or inspector. Do not build from this draft.** [V] verified, [E] estimate or trade practice. T1 owner-occupied, T2 spec. Assumed build: sloping-to-flat lot, septic, public water or well, unincorporated Kitsap County.

**Outcome:** when you finish this module you will have a cleared, drained, accessible site with a rough-graded building pad, erosion control in place, and utilities routed or installed.

**Before you start:** issued building permit and conditions (5.10), approved site plan and erosion plan, septic design, utility estimates, insurance in force (Module 8), loan recorded if financed (7.7).

**Sequence note:** the septic system (11.11) and service trenches (11.12–11.13) can be installed now or after framing. Doing them later keeps heavy trucks off finished work but leaves trenches open in wetter months. Decide in 9.4. Both must be complete before final.

**Inspections in this module:** temporary power (state, 11.5); erosion control; septic (health district); water service and any plumbing in trenches; electrical trench (state).

**Track note:** on T2, your own equipment work on a house built for sale falls under the contractor rules in Module 2.

---

### 11.1 Walk the site and mark limits
- **What:** clearing limits, protected trees, buffers, septic areas, and the driveway line flagged on the ground.
- **How:** carry the approved site plan. Flag clearing limits in one color, protected areas in another, drainfield and reserve in a third. Stake property corners.
- **Standard:** nothing outside the clearing limits is touched. Buffers and critical areas are checked by the inspector [V].
- **Crew, time:** 2 people, half a day.
- **If it goes wrong:** clearing strays into a buffer. Stop, photograph, and call the county before they call you.

### 11.2 Call utility locates (wait)
- **What:** marked underground utilities and a ticket number.
- **How:** request locates through the one-call service before any digging. Private lines past the meter are not marked; hire a private locator if the site has prior development.
- **Standard:** outline the dig area in white paint, then give notice at least two full business days before digging, not counting the day you call. Marks expire 45 days after the notice, so renew for long jobs. Within 24 in. of a marked line, expose the utility by hand before using a machine [V]. Wait for all utilities to respond.

### 11.3 Install erosion control and the construction entrance
- **What:** silt fence or wattles on the downhill side, a rock construction entrance, and covered stockpile locations.
- **How:** follow the approved erosion plan. Trench the bottom of silt fence into the ground. Lay quarry rock over fabric at the entrance.
- **Standard:** erosion control is a permit requirement; the first inspection can be requested with the footing and setback inspection, and it is checked throughout [V].
- **Crew, time, tools:** 2 people, 1 day; trencher or excavator, stakes, fabric, rock.
- **If it goes wrong:** muddy water leaves the site. That draws a stop-work order faster than almost anything else.

### 11.4 Set up temporary power and facilities (wait)
- **What:** temporary power pole or generator, portable toilet, locked storage, dumpster, and the plan box.
- **How:** order temporary service from the utility early; it needs a state electrical permit and inspection before the utility connects [E].
- **Options:** temporary utility power (quiet, cheap to run, slow to get); generator (immediate, fuel and noise). Many owner-builders start on a generator and switch.
- **Track:** T1 owners may install the temporary service under an owner permit; T2 uses a licensed electrician.

### 11.5 Pass the temporary power inspection (wait)
- **What:** state electrical approval of the temporary power pole, which the utility needs before it will energize the service.
- **How:** 1) Buy the state electrical permit before any electrical work starts. 2) Build the pole to the utility's written temporary service requirements. 3) Request the inspection through the state electrical program, not the county permit portal. 4) Once approved, tell the utility; it schedules the connection on its own timeline.
- **Standard:** electrical inspections in unincorporated Kitsap County are done by the state, not the county [E — confirm with the state electrical program for your address]. Nothing is energized before approval.
- **Options:** one permit covering temporary power, the service trench (11.13, 11.14), the grounding electrode (12.10), and the house wiring (Module 15); or separate permits for temporary and permanent work. Ask the state office which costs less and whether an owner permit can cover all of it.
- **Track:** T1 owner permit; T2 licensed electrician's permit (Module 2).
- **Wait:** inspection lead time plus the utility's connection lead time. Run on a generator meanwhile (11.4).
- **Done when:** approval is recorded and the utility has a connection date.
- **If it goes wrong:** the pole fails for grounding, height, or bracing. Fix to the utility's sheet and re-request; the utility will not connect on a verbal.

### 11.6 Clear and grub
- **What:** trees, stumps, brush, and organic topsoil removed from the building pad, driveway, and utility routes.
- **How:** fell and remove trees; pull stumps; strip topsoil and stockpile it for final landscaping (18.5).
- **Standard:** no organic material left under the house, driveway, or flatwork.
- **Safety:** tree felling near the building area or lines is specialist work. Hire it if in doubt.
- **Money out:** hauling or grinding; burning needs its own permission.
- **Later trades:** stockpiled topsoil saves buying it back.

### 11.7 Protect the septic and reserve areas
- **What:** a physical barrier around the drainfield and reserve area.
- **How:** fence it with stakes and tape or temporary fencing. Brief every driver.
- **Standard:** no vehicles, stockpiles, or excavation on these areas. Compacted or disturbed soil can void the approved design [E].
- **If it goes wrong:** a truck crosses the drainfield area. Tell the septic designer before installation; they decide whether the soil is still usable.

### 11.8 Rough grade and cut the driveway
- **What:** driveway subgrade cut and rocked for construction traffic; site sloped so water drains away from the pad.
- **How:** cut to the approved alignment and width; place fabric and base rock; shape swales per the erosion plan.
- **Standard:** fire access for single-family homes requires a road at least 20 ft wide with an approved turnaround [V]. Your approved plan governs the driveway itself. A new driveway connection needs a road approach permit [V].
- **Crew, time, tools:** 1 operator plus a grade checker; 1–3 days; excavator or dozer, roller or plate compactor, laser.
- **If it goes wrong:** the driveway is too steep or soft for a loaded concrete truck. Fix it before the pour date, not on it.

### 11.9 Rough-stake the house and set a benchmark
- **What:** four corner stakes offset from the building, and a marked elevation benchmark.
- **Options:** surveyor stakes (best near setbacks or buffers) or your own layout from property corners.
- **Standard:** offsets far enough out to survive excavation. The precise layout is step 12.5.

### 11.10 Excavate the building pad
- **What:** the pad cut to rough grade for the crawl space, at least 2 to 3 ft wider than the outside of the footings all round, so there is room to set, brace, and strip forms and to lay the footing drain [E].
- **How:** dig to the planned elevation using the laser; keep the bottom undisturbed; stockpile suitable backfill separately from topsoil.
- **Standard:** footings must bear on firm, undisturbed soil; over-excavated or filled areas need a compaction report [V]. Stop above final footing grade; footings are dug to final depth in 12.7.
- **Safety:** slope the sides of any cut people will work beside. Under Washington rules anyone working in a trench or beside a cut 4 ft or deeper needs sloping, benching, or shoring [V]; keep spoils at least 2 ft back from the edge [E].
- **If it goes wrong:** you find soft soil, fill, or water. Stop and call the engineer or a geotechnical engineer. This is what the contingency is for.

### 11.11 Install the septic system (wait)
- **What:** tank, piping, and drainfield installed to the approved design and inspected before cover.
- **How:** 1) Obtain the installation permit (needs the building permit number) [V]. 2) Install tank and components at designed elevations. 3) Leave everything exposed. 4) Have the designer request the pre-cover inspection and supply the red-lined site plan; the health district inspects within two business days of that request [V]. 5) Cover after approval.
- **Standard:** a health district employee inspects the system before it is covered [V].
- **Options:** certified septic installer (normal, and what lenders expect); or owner installation, which the health district may allow only for the resident owner of a single-family residence, meaning someone who lives there or intends to for at least two years [V]. Conditions include: a standard gravity system with an approved reserve area; drainfield and reserve not within 200 ft of marine water or 100 ft of surface water; the designer of record agrees to oversee the work; a competency meeting with health district staff and a signed homeowner installation agreement; no other owner-installed system in the past two years; any hired help must be a certified installer or a licensed designer or engineer [V]. T2 cannot use this route.
- **Recommendation:** hire a certified installer. Elevation errors of an inch matter and the system is expensive to redo.
- **Later trades:** leave the building sewer stub where the plumber needs it (12.2 sleeve location).

### 11.12 Bring in water
- **What:** well drilled and tested, or the water service line laid from the meter to the house.
- **How (service line):** trench, bed the pipe in select fill, install tracer wire with non-metallic pipe, pressure test, inspect, backfill.
- **Standard:** minimum 3/4 in. service; burial at least 18 in. or 12 in. below frost depth per jurisdiction; water line at least 12 in. above the top of a sewer in the same trench and on a solid shelf 12 in. to the side; select backfill 6 in. below and 12 in. above the pipe with no rock over 3/4 in.; test at working pressure or 50 psi for 15 minutes [V].
- **Options (well):** licensed driller only. Get the well log and a water quality test for the health district.
- **Track:** T2 water service is plumbing work for a licensed plumber.

### 11.13 Trench power and telecom
- **What:** conduit installed from the utility connection point to the meter location, with pull strings.
- **How:** follow the utility's written requirements for conduit size, depth, sweeps, and separation from other lines. Leave the trench open for inspection.
- **Standard:** the state electrical permit lists a ditch cover inspection [V]. Depth and conduit specifics come from the utility and the electrical code; get them in writing.
- **Later trades:** add a spare conduit for future use (vehicle charging, a shop, fiber). It costs little now.

### 11.14 Pass utility inspections (wait)
- **What:** approvals for trenches and services before backfill.
- **How:** request each inspection with the trench open and pipe on test where required. The power trench is inspected by the state under the electrical permit from 11.5; water and any plumbing in trenches by the county; septic by the health district.
- **Standard:** nothing is backfilled without approval. Photograph every trench with a tape for scale and note its depth and offset from the house.

### 11.15 Stabilize the site
- **What:** exposed soil covered, stockpiles tarped, entrance rock refreshed, drainage working.
- **Standard:** the site should survive a heavy rain without muddy runoff leaving it.
- **Crew, time:** 2 people, half a day, repeated as needed.

### 11.16 Close out
- **What:** as-built utility sketch, inspection approvals, well log or water documentation, updated cost report and schedule.
- **Output:** utility as-built drawing with depths and offsets.

## Top mistakes
1. Disturbing soil before erosion control (11.3).
2. Driving on the drainfield (11.7).
3. Backfilling before inspection (11.14).
4. A driveway that cannot take a concrete truck (11.8).
5. No record of where utilities are buried (11.16).

## Call scripts
- **Power utility:** "I have a building permit at [address]. Please send your written trench, conduit, and meter base requirements for new residential service, and tell me your current lead time for connection."
- **Health district:** "My septic design is approved under [number] and my building permit is [number]. What do I need to obtain the installation permit, who may install, and how do I request the pre-cover inspection?"

- Whether an owner may install the septic system, and tank and drainfield tolerances.
- Utility conduit and trench specifications.
- Whether burning or stump disposal needs separate permission.
- Frost depth for water service burial on your lot.
