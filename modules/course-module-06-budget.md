# Module 6 — Budget and Estimating

**Draft v1 — 15 Nov 2026 — not yet reviewed by a professional.** [V] verified, [E] estimate. T1 owner-occupied, T2 spec.

**Outcome:** when you finish this module you will have a line-item baseline budget built from your own plans and real quotes, a contingency, and a weekly routine that tells you the cost to complete.

**Before you start:** frozen plans (Module 4), site development estimate (3.10), permit fee figures (5.10), legal scope matrix (Module 2).

**Principle:** a budget built from cost per square foot is a guess. A budget built from quantities and quotes is a plan.

---

### 6.1 Set up cost codes
- **What:** a numbered list of budget lines used everywhere: bids, receipts, bookkeeping, draws.
- **How:** use one line per phase and trade, in build order. A workable set:
    - 01 Land and closing · 02 Design and engineering · 03 Permits and fees · 04 Utilities and connections · 05 Site work · 06 Septic · 07 Foundation · 08 Framing lumber and hardware · 09 Trusses · 10 Roofing · 11 Windows and exterior doors · 12 Siding and exterior trim · 13 Plumbing · 14 Electrical · 15 Heating and ventilation · 16 Insulation and air sealing · 17 Drywall · 18 Interior doors and trim · 19 Cabinets and counters · 20 Flooring and tile · 21 Paint · 22 Appliances and fixtures · 23 Flatwork · 24 Landscaping · 25 Tools, rentals, and consumables · 26 Insurance · 27 Financing, interest, and property tax · 28 Temporary facilities and waste · 29 Sales costs and excise tax [T2] · 30 Contingency
- **Standard:** if your lender has a draw format, map your codes to it now (Module 7). Enter every line with sales tax included.
- **Output:** cost code list.

### 6.2 Take off quantities
- **What:** counts and measures of every material, from the plans.
- **How:** work sheet by sheet: concrete in cubic yards, rebar in feet, studs and plates by length, sheathing by sheet, trusses by count, roofing by square (100 sq ft), siding by square foot, windows and doors from the schedule, drywall by sheet, insulation by square foot, flooring by room.
- **Options:** by hand on printed plans; with takeoff software; or ask the lumber yard and truss supplier for a takeoff with their quote (free, but check it).
- **Standard:** add waste: about 10% on framing lumber, sheathing, and drywall; 5–10% on concrete; 10–15% on tile and flooring with pattern [E].
- **Done when:** a second person spot-checks ten lines against the plans.

### 6.3 Price materials
- **What:** supplier quotes against your takeoff.
- **How:** send the same list to two or three suppliers. Ask for delivered pricing, how long the quote holds, and return terms.
- **Standard:** lumber and sheet goods quotes may hold only days to weeks [E]. Note the quote date on each line.
- **Standard:** both tracks pay sales tax on materials, tools, and rentals at the rate for the delivery address: 9.2% in unincorporated Kitsap County in late 2026 [V]. A T2 builder cannot buy materials with a reseller permit [V]. Ask whether each quote includes tax and enter every line tax-included. Anything bought without Washington sales tax, such as some online or out-of-state orders, owes use tax at the same rate [V]; budget it.

### 6.4 Get bids for hired scopes (wait)
- **What:** at least two written bids for each scope you will hire.
- **How:** use the bid package process in Module 10. For scopes you plan to do yourself, get one bid anyway as a check on your labor value and as a fallback.
- **Standard:** bids must state inclusions, exclusions, who pulls the permit, and sales tax as a separate amount. On both tracks the contractor must charge sales tax on its full price, labor and materials [V]. A bid that is silent on tax may be about 9% low.

### 6.5 Add soft costs
- **What:** every non-construction cost.
- **List:** design, engineering, survey, septic design, studies, permit and impact fees, utility connection charges, insurance, legal and accounting, portable toilet, dumpster, temporary power, tool purchases and rentals (with sales tax). Itemize loan costs: origination fee or points, appraisal, title insurance and escrow, recording, lender inspection fee for each draw, and any extension fee [E]. Add land closing costs if not already in code 01.
- **Standard:** permit and impact fees for the county's 2,000 sq ft example total about $15,500 [V].
- **If it goes wrong:** soft costs are forgotten and found one invoice at a time. This list is the fix.

### 6.6 Add carrying costs
- **What:** costs that accrue with time: loan interest, property tax, insurance, utilities, and your current housing if it overlaps.
- **How:** multiply monthly cost by the schedule in months, then add three months [E]. Interest: you pay only on what has been drawn, so estimate loan amount × annual rate × years of construction × about 0.5 to 0.6, then ask whether the loan funds an interest reserve or you pay monthly from cash [E]. Property tax: the land is taxed throughout. The assessor adds new construction at its value as of 31 July each year for tax due the following year, so a build that spans a 31 July carries tax on a partly built house the next year [V].
- **Track:** T2 carries through the sale closing, not just completion.

### 6.7 Value owner labor and set contingency
- **What:** a contingency line, and a record of what your labor is replacing.
- **How:** 1) For each self-performed scope, record the fallback bid from 6.4. 2) Set contingency as a percentage of hard costs.
- **Standard:** 15% contingency for a first owner-built house and 20% on a sloped, wet, or unserviced lot; the 10% in the module gate is a floor, not a target, and is what an experienced builder carries on a known site [E]. Site work, septic, and utilities are where first builds overrun, so do not release any contingency until Module 11 is closed out [E]. Keep owner labor out of the cash budget: show it in a separate column at the fallback bid value. Expect lenders to count it at zero as a source of funds; Fannie Mae's rule is that sweat equity is generally not an acceptable source for down payment, closing costs, or reserves [V]. It can still help indirectly, because the appraisal values the finished house and not your cash cost (7.5). Owner labor adds nothing to your tax basis [V].
- **If it goes wrong:** you become unavailable and must hire a scope. The fallback bids tell you the exposure; the sum of your two largest is a sensible stress test.

### 6.8 Assemble the baseline budget
- **What:** one sheet: code, description, quantity, unit cost, total, source (quote, bid, estimate), and date.
- **Standard:** fewer than 20% of dollars should rest on estimates [E]. Everything else has a quote or bid behind it.
- **Done when:** total is at or under the ceiling from 1.4 with contingency intact.

### 6.9 Cut cost if over the ceiling
- **What:** a list of changes that brings the total under the ceiling.
- **Order of cuts [E]:** 1) area; 2) shape and roof complexity; 3) site costs (driveway length, hard surface); 4) window count and sizes; 5) finishes. Do not cut structure, water management, air sealing, or contingency.
- **Standard:** changes to plans after freeze need a permit revision if already submitted.
- **If it goes wrong:** you cut contingency to make it fit. That is not a cut; it is a deferred overrun.

### 6.10 Set up weekly cost tracking
- **What:** a weekly routine and report.
- **How:** every week: 1) enter receipts by code; 2) update committed costs (signed bids, orders placed); 3) estimate cost to complete each line; 4) compare projected final to baseline; 5) record any draw on contingency with a reason.
- **Standard:** projected final cost = spent + committed but not yet paid + estimate to complete the work not yet committed. Count each dollar once: when you pay against a signed bid, move that amount from committed to spent. Track that number, not just spending.
- **Crew, time:** 1 person, 1 hour a week.
- **Output:** weekly cost report; change log.

## Module gate
- [ ] Baseline budget at or under the ceiling
- [ ] At least 80% of dollars backed by quotes or bids
- [ ] Contingency of 10% or more, untouched
- [ ] Fallback bids recorded for major self-performed scopes
- [ ] Weekly cost routine started

## Top mistakes
1. Budgeting from cost per square foot (6.2).
2. Forgetting soft and carrying costs (6.5, 6.6).
3. Tracking what was spent but not the cost to complete (6.10).
4. Spending contingency on upgrades (6.9, 6.10).
