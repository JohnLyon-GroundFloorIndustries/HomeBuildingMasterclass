# Owner-Builder Masterclass — Design Brief and Step Map

**15 Nov 2026 — 21 modules, 264 steps. Matches the web page after the specialist review round and the five added steps. Not reviewed by a working professional. Do not build from it yet.**

## Design brief

### The transformation
- **T1:** from "we could probably build a house" to living in a permitted, inspected house you built, with records that prove how.
- **T2:** from "we could flip our skills into profit" to a sold house, a clean set of books, and a real number for what your labor earned.

### Course outcomes
By the end, the learner can:

1. Decide go or no-go from numbers, and pick a track knowing its legal limits.
2. Buy a lot only after proving it is buildable at a known cost.
3. Produce permit-ready plans that fit the lot, the budget, and their own skills.
4. Hold a line-item budget and schedule and keep both current weekly.
5. Hire, contract, and pay trades without disputes or liens.
6. Build each phase to the standard an inspector and a good framer expect, and pass each inspection.
7. Obtain occupancy and either move in or sell with complete records.
8. Review the project and decide whether to do it again.

### Learner profile
- **Knows:** tools, equipment operation, basic carpentry and concrete, reading a tape and a level.
- **Wrongly believes:** the hard part is the physical work; codes are suggestions inspectors negotiate; cost per square foot is a budget; a spec house is the same job with nicer photos.
- **Where capable DIYers fail:** paperwork sequence (septic before permit, permit before dirt), embeds and blocking for later trades, water management details, working at professional speed on paper only, and carrying costs.

### Assumed build
About 2,000 sq ft, single story, wood frame on a crawl space, all-electric with a heat pump, composition roof, fiber-cement siding, on septic, in unincorporated Kitsap County, Washington. Variants are noted where they change a step.

### Pacing
Lessons are taken just before the matching build phase. Part A (modules 1–10) is completed before breaking ground. Learner study time is about 150–200 hours across the course [E], separate from build labor.

### Project binder
One working file, built step by step: decisions, approvals, budget, schedule, contracts, lien waivers, inspection results, photos before cover, as-builts, warranties. Every step names what it adds.

### Format mix
- Reference tutorial (this text) for every step.
- Filmed demonstration for hand skills: layout, rebar, framing layout, flashing, air sealing, tile waterproofing.
- Worksheets and templates for every planning step.
- Expert interviews: attorney, CPA, lender, inspector, septic designer.

### Gates
Each module ends with a gate. Three are hard stops before money is committed: end of Module 1 (go/no-go), end of Module 3 (close on land), end of Module 7 (loan closing or cash committed).

## Points of no return

Closing on land (3.14) · permit submittal (5.8) · loan closing (7.7) · footing pour (12.12) · wall pour (12.17) · roof sheathing over trusses (13.17) · covering walls with drywall (16.8) · tile over waterproofing (17.5) · flatwork pour (18.3) · sale closing (20.13).

## Production plan

1. **Build first:** Modules 1–3 and 5. They decide whether money is spent.
2. **Guest experts needed:** attorney (2), CPA (2, 20), lender (7), septic designer (3), inspector (12–19), electrician (15), plumber (15).
3. **Filmed demonstrations:** 12 (layout, rebar, embeds), 13 (layout, raising walls, trusses), 14 (window flashing), 15 (drain slopes, tests), 16 (air sealing), 17 (shower waterproofing).
4. **Review order for professionals:** 12, 13, 15 first (structure and life safety), then 14 and 16 (water and energy), then the rest.

## Open questions

1. Which track will the first build follow?
2. City lot or unincorporated county? Sewer or septic? Public water or well?
3. Crawl space, slab, or basement?
4. Cash, construction loan, or VA construction loan?
5. Will the course stay personal, or be published for others? Publication needs attorney review and state-by-state scoping.
