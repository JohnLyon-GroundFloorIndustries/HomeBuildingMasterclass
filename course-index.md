## Assumptions the course is built on

- About 2,000 sq ft, single story, wood frame on a crawl space, all-electric with a heat pump, composition roof, fiber-cement siding.
- Unincorporated Kitsap County, Washington, on septic.
- Both tracks: owner-occupied (T1) and spec (T2), marked in every module.
- Slab, basement, sewer, and city-lot variants are noted where they change a step but are not written out.

## How it was made, and what that means

- Each module was written from the step map, then reviewed by the same AI author in the same working session.
- **A second, independent round followed on 15 Nov 2026.** Five AI reviewers, standing in for a builder, a structural engineer, a county inspector, a Washington construction attorney, and a CPA, checked their modules against current sources. They proposed 125 corrections; 123 were applied and 2 were dropped as duplicates.
- **Five steps were added after that round, at the reviewers' request:** temporary power inspection (11.5), crawl space water (12.21), garage slab (12.24), scaffold and staging (13.15), eave and rake framing (13.16). They were written by the author and have not been through a review round; their standards are marked [E].
- **Neither round is a professional review.** AI reviewers can misread a statute or a code table. A licensed builder, engineer, attorney, and CPA still need to check the parts that are theirs.
- Facts marked [V] were checked against a cited source on 15 Nov 2026. Facts marked [E] are estimates or trade practice and were not sourced.

## What is solid

- **Washington licensing limits** on owner-performed work for a house built for sale (Module 2), from the statutes and state forms.
- **Local process:** health district septic approval, stormwater worksheet, road approach, fees, online-only applications (Modules 3, 5).
- **Inspection content** for foundation, shear, plumbing rough-in, insulation, and final, taken from county and regional inspection checklists (Modules 12–19).
- **State tax and lien basics** for spec builders and for paying trades (Modules 2, 10, 20).

## Known limits

1. **Code edition.** Washington is on the 2021 residential, energy, and plumbing codes, with the 2024 codes scheduled for 3 May 2027; the electrical code is the 2023 edition, with the 2026 edition from 31 December 2026. Several county checklists the course cites are still written to the 2018 codes. Not every value was re-checked against the codes in force.
2. **Electrical.** Module 15 gives sequence and coordination, not wiring rules. A self-wiring owner needs the adopted electrical code or a paid consult.
3. **Craft tolerances, durations, and costs** are estimates throughout.
4. **Financing (Module 7)** is general practice; lender rules vary bank by bank and none were verified. The VA construction loan change was read from third-party copies of VA guidance.
5. **Disclosure and warranty law on sale, and all federal tax**, need the attorney and CPA.
6. **Still not verified:** utility trench specifications (11.13); whether the state inspects temporary power and trenches at this address (11.5); whether one house on an existing lot is exempt from concurrency (5.8); whether an LLC can use the owner exemptions; whether helpers may assist on owner electrical work; whether roof nailing is inspected before underlayment (13.18); several plumbing values sourced only to 2018-code checklists; garage slab inspection and slope requirements (12.24).
7. **Source links:** some county checklist links returned "file not found" for two reviewers and have not been re-checked.
8. **Variants not written:** slab and basement foundations, prescriptive wall bracing, exterior continuous insulation details, two-story framing, gas appliances, sewer connection, city permitting.
9. **Not yet produced:** the templates each module names (worksheets, bid forms, checklists), photographs, and any filmed demonstrations.

## Open decisions

1. Which track the first real build follows.
2. Lot type: city or county, sewer or septic, public water or well.
3. Foundation type.
4. Funding: cash, construction loan, or VA construction loan.
5. Whether the course stays personal or is published. Publication needs attorney review and scoping by state.
