## The corrections that matter most

### Legal (attorney)
1. **Owner exemption misread.** The first draft said the owner exemption from contractor registration is lost only if the property is sold within twelve months of ownership. That limit applies to an owner who hires a registered contractor. An owner doing the work loses the exemption whenever the work is done in order to sell or lease, with no time limit. Separately, offering a house for sale before occupying it more than one year after substantial completion makes the seller a contractor by definition. "Build as owner-occupant, wait, then sell" is not a spec strategy.
2. **Lien notices.** Trades and suppliers hired directly by the owner, their direct subcontractors, and laborers can lien without sending any notice. A notice log alone misses most claimants; waivers must be collected against a list of everyone paid.
3. **Sale of a new house.** New construction is not exempt from the seller disclosure statement. An implied warranty of habitability can apply to a builder-vendor. Defect claims follow a statutory notice and cure process, with a six-year outer limit.
4. **Helpers.** An owner who directly hires workers for new construction must provide workers' compensation.

### Tax and money (CPA)
5. **Sales tax applies on both tracks**, on materials and on hired contractors' full price including labor: 9.2% in unincorporated Kitsap County in late 2026. The first draft mentioned it only for spec builds. Use tax applies to untaxed purchases.
6. **Federal rules now stated:** own labor is not part of cost basis; the home-sale exclusion needs two years of actual use; spec profit is ordinary income with possible self-employment tax; the homeowner energy credits ended after 2025 and the builder credit after June 2026.
7. **VA construction loans:** the builder identification requirement was dropped in March 2025, so lender policy, not VA rules, is now the barrier for a veteran acting as their own builder. Read from third-party copies of VA guidance; confirm with a lender.
8. **Cost-to-complete formula** double counted unless "committed" means unpaid commitments only.

### Structure (engineer)
9. **Code minimums were presented as the build standard.** Half-inch anchor bolts at 6 ft and a 24-in. top plate splice are floors. Engineered shear walls in this seismic category usually need more, and plate washers are required on every bolt in a braced wall line.
10. **Foundation reinforcing was incomplete:** the prescriptive minimum also needs a horizontal bar near the wall top, one near the footing bottom, and hooked dowels into the wall.
11. **Shear wall and truss rules:** the overdriven-nail rule was misstated; never altering a truss is a code requirement, not trade practice.
12. **Kitsap design criteria confirmed:** seismic category D2, 12 in. frost depth, 25 psf snow, 110 mph wind.

### Code and inspections (inspector)
13. **Blower door limit is 4.0 air changes per hour**, not 5, on every compliance path; air-leakage credits also require a heat recovery ventilator.
14. **Every ducted heating or cooling system is leak-tested**, not only ducts outside conditioned space.
15. **Grounding electrode:** inspected by the state electrical inspector, so the electrical permit must be bought before the footing pour.
16. **Toilet front clearance is 24 in.** under the Washington amendment, not 21. New-work water heater relief drains discharge outside.
17. **Code status:** 2021 residential, energy, and plumbing codes in force; 2024 codes scheduled for 3 May 2027; electrical code is the 2023 edition, with the 2026 edition from 31 December 2026.
18. **Fees:** the county's 2026 example totals $15,518 (was $15,083 for 2025). Plumbing and mechanical are inside the building permit.
19. **Inspection sequence** gained the grounding electrode, gypsum board, and health district final inspections.

### Construction (builder)
20. **Truss setting and fall protection** now carry bracing spacings and Washington's 4 ft fall-protection and trench thresholds.
21. **Fascia and eave blocking** were out of sequence or missing; now their own step (13.16).
22. **Schedule:** framing to dry-in is 14–26 weeks for a family crew, not 8–16. The course now tells the family to consider hiring a framing crew for the shell if it cannot fit the dry season.
23. **Crawl space drainage** was understated; now its own step (12.21).
